What happened: On June 25, 2026, the Supreme Court ruled the Trump administration could end Temporary Protected Status for Haiti and Syria. Haiti's TPS designation was formally terminated July 27, 2026, ending work authorization for approximately 350,000 people.

NPR, July 27, 2026
After July 27, thousands of TPS holders faced loss of legal status and potential deportation. The American Immigration Council noted that once TPS recipients lose status, they are at risk of arrest by ICE and may face immigration court proceedings. The economic impacts are likely significant in communities with high concentrations of TPS recipients. The administration encouraged TPS holders to return to Haiti.

Supreme Court Permits Trump to End Haiti, Syria TPS; 350,000+ Face Uncertain Status
National Immigration Forum & American Immigration Council, June-July 2026
On June 25, 2026, the Supreme Court ruled in Mullin v. Doe that the Secretary of Homeland Security's decisions to terminate TPS for Syria and Haiti are not judicially reviewable, effectively permitting the Trump administration to end TPS for both countries. In total, federal courts had halted TPS terminations for 7 countries (Burma, Ethiopia, Haiti, Somalia, South Sudan, Syria, Yemen) via court order before the Supreme Court decision.
