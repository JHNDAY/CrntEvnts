Trump vs. Buchanan: The Bottom of the Barrel
Trump and James Buchanan are competing for the title of worst president in American history. Buchanan, despite having served as Secretary of State, Ambassador to England and Russia, and House member, had terrible character. Trump faces similar character questions alongside his presidential record.

Both FDR and Trump started with similar aristocratic advantages. Yet FDR became one of America's best presidents while Trump is fighting it out with Buchanan and Andrew Johnson for the worst. Character failures appear to be the common thread.

03 Federalism & Local Governance Policy framework ▼
FDR's Coalition: Compromise as Governance
FDR held together a coalition including Southern Democrats who endorsed lynching of Black people. He couldn't pass a federal anti-lynching law, but he maintained coalition stability and achieved significantly positive results for the nation. This illustrates the tension between purity and pragmatism in federalism.
NYTThe Atlantic
Federal Rules Protecting Disadvantaged Groups
Federal mandates have historically been essential: women's voting rights, Black voting rights, and disability protections all required national enforcement when local jurisdictions refused to grant them. The challenge is determining which issues demand national standards vs. local control.
DOJ
Acemoglu on Local Decision-Making
The literature suggests that local government should make decisions on many issues, even if those decisions are initially wrong. Federalism requires surrendering centralized power, but it's more reliable and gives people ownership over outcomes.
Acemoglu OpEd
Over-Regulation Stifles Implementation
Democrats passed significant legislation but wrapped implementation with so many restrictions and red tape that projects sat idle. The Affordable Care Act worked, but public messaging failed. Biden's infrastructure bills couldn't get off the ground. Red tape defeats itself.
NYT
Sources: NYT The Atlantic DOJ
04 National vs. Local Authority Exercise in federalism ▼
Non-Negotiable National Standards
You cannot allow a municipality to say no Muslims can live here. You cannot allow states to deny voting rights based on race or gender. Some issues are truly non-negotiable and demand national enforcement to protect human dignity and fundamental rights.
DOJ
Issues Better Resolved Locally
Many policy areas benefit from local experimentation and learning. School curriculum, local zoning, environmental management, and economic development often produce better outcomes through local governance. Progress may be slower but more reliable and legitimate.
Brookings
Lack of Democratic Party Platform
The Democratic Party has struggled to articulate a coherent platform beyond opposition to Trump. Party platforms specify economic proposals and governing philosophy. Without one, voters lack clarity on what Democrats stand for—a significant weakness against organized opposition.
DNC
Surrendering Centralized Power
True federalism requires elites to surrender control and accept local outcomes they may not prefer. "I know what should be done—just listen to us" is the opposite of federalism. The challenge is accepting that communities will sometimes choose differently.
Acemoglu OpEd
Sources: DOJ Brookings DNC
05 Economic Turmoil & Bond Markets Market stress ▼
Interest Rate Inversion and Treasury Crisis
Long-term interest rates are now roughly equal to short-term Treasury yields—an inversion that signals economic trouble. The government must offer Treasury bonds at 4.5% to 5% to attract buyers. This high interest expense crowds out funding for vital programs.
TreasuryCNBC
AI Boom Creating Market Distortion
AI investment has been highly competitive with U.S. Treasury bonds. Investors can get high returns in AI, so Treasury must match those rates. This artificially high rate environment is tied to AI speculation, not fundamentals. When the AI boom corrects, rates may reset.
CNBCNYT
Economy is Extraordinarily Complex
The stock market is doing fine based on speculation that AI will solve all problems—which it will not. A reckoning is coming. Like weather, the economy and its movements are best understood afterwards with historical perspective. Predicting outcomes in real time is nearly impossible.
NYTWSJ
Rough Economic Performance Ahead
The market is in turmoil. With high interest rates, government expenses for servicing debt will consume resources that could go to programs. Rough economic performance is likely in the medium term, contingent on how the AI boom resolves.
Moody's Analytics
Sources: U.S. Treasury CNBC NYT Moody's
06 Energy Costs & Global Impact Supply chain risk ▼
Iran War Costs: $1,000 Per Household
Moody's Analytics has calculated that the Iran war, which affects energy prices globally, has already cost the U.S. economy approximately $1,000 per household in just the first six months of conflict. This impact cascades through fertilizer, food production, and supply chains.
Moody's
Energy Price Cascades Through Food
Rising energy prices directly affect fertilizer costs, which affect global crop production. Food prices from domestic and worldwide crops will rise. We're not isolated; we depend on world crops everywhere. The potential impact on the price of milk, grains, and other staples is enormous.
USDAFAO
Individual Preparation Uncertain
The question for individuals becomes: do I need to stockpile rice now because it will be too expensive or unavailable later? The answer depends on how fast the supply chain breaks down. Preparation is uncertain in complex, interdependent systems.
USDA
Masking Underlying Problems
AI boom activity has been carrying the economy for a long time, masking these underlying energy and inflation problems. When that boom corrects, the full impact of geopolitical energy disruption becomes visible. Timing and severity are unpredictable.
NYT
Sources: Moody's USDA FAO NYT
07 Big Tent Coalition Politics Strategic analysis ▼
Working-Class Voters Left Behind
Acemoglu's OpEd in the NYT argues for working-class liberalism. Working-class voters have been left behind by economic growth. People with college degrees have benefited from the shift toward technical expertise. This is why many voted for Trump—they felt ignored, even though Biden passed legislation that would have helped them.
Acemoglu OpEd
Adversarial Populism Alienates the Middle
Framing everything as oppressor versus oppressed alienates the middle class and prevents coalition building. The risk is that populism becomes an end in itself rather than a means to power. Slogans and grievance don't build sustainable coalitions.
Acemoglu OpEd
FDR Model: Shared Prosperity Agenda
Historically, FDR united the New Deal Brain Trust, the labor movement, and conservative Southern Democrats through a shared prosperity agenda. Modern Democrats need a big tent including both traditional progressives and working-class voters. Shared goals beat ideological purity.
Acemoglu OpEdNYT
Feasible Policy + Progressive Vision
Conservatives are right that governance requires feasible policy, not just slogans. Progressives are right that we need new ideas on inequality, opportunity, and corporate excess. Success requires both pragmatic implementation AND progressive vision working together.
Acemoglu OpEd
Sources: Acemoglu OpEd NYT
