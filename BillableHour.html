AI Puts the Billable Hour on Trial

Nearly nine in ten U.K. and Irish lawyers now use AI. It is changing how firms charge, how much they can take on, and how young lawyers learn the trade.

By Lindsay Dodgson · Visual summary of the CNBC report

Artificial intelligence has reached almost 90% of legal professionals in the U.K. and Ireland, according to legal software company Clio's 2026 Legal Insights Report. Among firms using it, almost 80% say they handle more work without adding resources, and over 70% say it has cut costs by absorbing administrative work once done by support staff.

That success creates a problem for one of the profession's oldest conventions. Many firms make their money by the hour. When a machine shrinks hours of document review and drafting into minutes, the old arithmetic stops working.

You can't charge 16 hours for something that takes 16 seconds.Nick Rowles-Davies · Founder & CEO, Lexolent

The biggest U.K. firms are already moving. A&O Shearman built AI agents with legal AI company Harvey to review loan agreements and analyze regulatory filings, finishing in minutes work that once took several hours. Slaughter and May rolled out Harvey across every practice area this year.

The billable hour is not vanishing overnight, says Mansoor Soomro of Teesside University's Future of Work Research Unit, but it is getting harder to justify.

Inside the Story

Three pressures on the profession

I · The Business Model

Routine Work Goes First

Standard documents where a lawyer only fills in details are the most exposed, says Rowles-Davies. That is close to an automatic process.

Complex matters still need human judgment, especially in reading AI output and choosing a client's strategy. Lawyers must also catch hallucinations; some have been sanctioned for citing cases that do not exist.

The billable hour isn't disappearing overnight, but it's getting harder to justify.Paraphrasing Mansoor Soomro, Teesside University
II · Training the Next Generation

Who Teaches the Juniors?

Grunt work used to be the classroom. Reviewing hundreds of documents taught what matters; drafting contracts built writing skill; researching cases built legal reasoning.

With AI doing that work, firms must replace those lessons on purpose. A&O Shearman now prizes curiosity and entrepreneurship, and is building careers in legal engineering, product and AI delivery.

"A faster route to a draft does not automatically mean a faster route to competence."Jorge Bestard, Harvey
III · The Working Day

Will the Time Come Back?

Whether lawyers' lives improve depends on what firms do with the hours AI frees up. Today many work evenings and weekends they would rather not.

Soomro expects judgment to become far more valuable, with the edge going to lawyers who can connect several areas of law to solve a complex problem, not narrow specialists.

"Lawyers [are] telling us that their day is getting better."Joshua Lenon, Clio
