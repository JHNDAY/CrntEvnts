The Pre-Election Cake Is Nearly Baked

With the midterms exactly one month away, several things are already set. There is essentially no chance that the Iran War can be resolved before the midterms. The Iranians are keenly aware of this timing, and Trump is now saying, and has backed with increased force deployment, that the war will not be resolved until after the midterms.

The September jobs report showed a disappointing 29k new jobs and a 0.1% uptick in unemployment to 4.2%. The October jobs report is not scheduled for release until after Election Day, which leaves the September inflation report, due 10/14, as the last major economic report before the midterms. Year-over-year inflation should stay near last month's 3.4%, compared with 2.9% for the last full month of the Biden administration.

Gallup's finding that a record 89% of respondents perceive widespread government corruption, up from 73% in 2024, suggests growing public awareness of the venality of Trump and his cohort. Midterm polling continues to be very unfavorable for Republicans, their candidates are now trying to differentiate themselves from Trump, and Republican campaign investments are shifting toward traditionally Red states. Efforts to restrict mail-in ballots have mostly been deterred by the courts, and given the adverse climate, the Republican gerrymanders Trump instigated may prove counterproductive.

Trump's Peevish TIME Interview

In his interview with TIME on Monday, Trump differed sharply from the assessment above. A sampling:

On the Iran War: The initial timeframe was extended because "I wanted to go further" in diminishing Iran. He bragged, "They're doing very poorly. Their economy is gone. Their inflation is at 300% or something like that. Their navy is gone. Their air force is gone. Everything's gone, and they want to make a deal, but I want to make the real deal."

On inflation and affordability: "When I took over, I inherited the greatest inflation in history. I inherited the greatest price hikes in history...I've got—I've gotten much of it down. The only thing I have to get down now is the gas, and almost everything else is coming down rapidly." (Ed. Note: Is he talking about his digestion, his verbiage, or the commodity?)

On the debt: Told that $11T of the $40T national debt arose during his two terms, he fell back on "Biden just killed our country," then added that "the one thing that you can do is pay it off through growth, and we've never had growth like this."

On his building projects: "The White House was a dump. The moldings were falling off the outside of the building. It hadn't been painted…it was just a disgraceful dump." On the Kennedy Center: "It's in terrible shape. I'd be willing to save it, but it's in terrible, terrible shape. Only I can save it," adding that if he does so, "I think the Trump Administration should be recognized."

On adverse polling: "They're fake numbers. I would beat anybody running today by 20 points," and "we have the strongest economy. We have the strongest industrial economy...We got more manufacturing jobs coming online than at any time in the history of our country."

On Republican candidates: "I simply think that we're going to do well. If I campaign for people, those people are going to win."

The disconnect between reality and public perception, on the one hand, and Trump's point of view, on the other, evokes in reverse Jack Nicholson's Colonel Nathan Jessep telling Tom Cruise's Lt. Daniel Kaffee "You can't handle the truth" in 1992's A Few Good Men. The extent to which Trump's remarks reflect self-delusion as opposed to mendacity is unfathomable.

$20 Million in Taxpayer-Funded Political "PSAs"

This week the administration launched a $20MM series of taxpayer-funded, overtly political "Public Service Announcements" lauding Trump's performance. The lines include "Together we will defeat communism, socialism and Marxism in America," "With you at my side, we will demolish the deep state," "expel the warmongers from our government," and "I need somebody with arms strong enough to rustle the deep state, and yet gentle enough to deliver his own grandchild. Somebody to ruffle the feathers," with a voiceover declaring, "So God made Trump."

Asked about the appropriateness of the ads, FCC Chair Brendan Carr said the series "doesn't strike me as anything at all out of the ordinary" and does not "merit any sort of FCC review."

Jack Smith Before the Senate Judiciary Committee

Jack Smith appeared publicly before the Senate Judiciary Committee for the first time, after testifying before the House Judiciary Committee in January. Highlights of his opening statement:

  • "My career has been dedicated to serving our country by upholding the rule of law and the core principles on which our country was founded."
  • "I believe that there is no role for politics in the proper administration of justice."
  • "Our investigation developed proof beyond a reasonable doubt that President Trump engaged in criminal activity."
  • "I will not be silenced by the continued threats of prosecution from the President or others."
  • "The rule of law faces challenges unlike any we have experienced in our lifetime."

Republicans castigated Smith for his investigations in general, and for obtaining content-free records of phone calls in particular:

Chairman Chuck Grassley: "This was rampant abuse of political targeting...There's no legitimate basis for what you did."

Sen. Ted Cruz (TX): "The most egregious abuse of power in the history of the DOJ."

Sen. Mike Lee (UT): Smith's probe "makes what Richard Nixon did in Watergate look like a Sunday picnic."

Sen. John Kennedy (LA): "You make me want to throw up in my mouth."

Sen. Eric Schmitt (MO): "You're the villain. You've done so much damage...You will go down as a total dirtbag."

Thinking he had a "gotcha" moment, Schmitt displayed a storyboard implying that Smith had clandestinely met Fulton County DA Fani Willis in February 2024 at an Atlanta Hawks game, on the eve of her admitting to an affair with Special Prosecutor Nathan Wade. "I don't think you know that we have this stuff, so I'll give you a second to process it so you don't perjure yourself," he said. After a moment of puzzlement, Smith replied that he may indeed have gone to a basketball game that night, but it was at the University of Maryland, when they played Iowa and its star Caitlin Clark.

Sen. Amy Klobuchar immediately saw that Smith had attended an Iowa Hawkeyes game, not an Atlanta Hawks game, and admonished Schmitt: "You don't call someone a dirtbag when you haven't done your own research and you don't have your facts straight." Schmitt's blunder was the most memorable takeaway from the hearing.

The Republican members were trying to erase the reality of Trump's misdeeds by attacking the special prosecutor as an unscrupulous political hack. Ironically, all their spurious allegations of judicial misconduct and vindictive prosecution describe Trump and his second-term DOJ to a tee.

Beyond the Seven:

  • Kansas: Methodist mega-church pastor Adam Hamilton (D) is running even with incumbent Roger Marshall (R), who has been dogged by the disclosure that his former OB-GYN practice filed over 700 lawsuits against patients for unpaid bills. Hamilton's favorability is net positive, Marshall's net negative. Republicans just shifted funding to Kansas from North Carolina, where their candidate Michael Whatley is badly trailing former Democratic governor Roy Cooper.
  • Minnesota: Lt. Gov. Peggy Flanagan (D) surprisingly leads former NFL sideline reporter Michele Tafoya by only 1 or 2%, perhaps reflecting the presumed Medicaid and child care fraud scandals.
  • Nebraska: Independent Dan Osborn is running even with Republican incumbent Pete Ricketts.
  • Texas: 150k more Democrats than Republicans voted in the primaries, whereas in 2018, 500k more Republicans did. Given Ken Paxton's struggles against James Talarico, incumbent Sen. John Cornyn remarked that "if they (the Republicans) actually wanted to hold the seat,...I would be a safer bet." Paxton privately said that the midterm convention in Dallas had hurt his campaign.

Where Republicans Are Spending: The Senate Leadership Fund is sending money to nine states for the last month, in descending order of funding: TX, OH, MI, ME, GA, IA, KS, NH, AK. Five of the nine have been solidly Red, two are Purple and two are light Blue.

More on the Kennedy Center

This week a whistleblower disclosed that Trump-appointed executive director Matt Floca halted a planned $9.3MM roof repair to address leaks before later saying the Center had to close because of those leaks. Emerging information indicates the Center was solvent, but after Trump took control, ticket income immediately fell by 25%, and contributions, which accounted for 60% of total income, fell by 40%, with things progressively worse this year. Arts executive Zannie Gahl-Mills commented, "It was unusual for an organization to have gone this far south, so fast."

In essence, Trump asphyxiated the Center and is blackmailing it to add his name to the facade. This year's Kennedy Center Honors will, ironically, be held on Pearl Harbor Day at Washington's Capital One Arena.

When the Senate attempted to ban the destruction of presidential memorials by unanimous consent, Sen. Mike Lee blocked the bipartisan effort. On the outrage across the greater D.C. community, Jen Rubin wrote that the takeover "has become one of the most visible and absurd displays of Trumpian ego and foolishness colliding with reality."

Project 2025 in Action
  • Pocket rescission: Through a legally dubious maneuver, the White House moved to cancel $810MM of congressionally approved funding. $567MM was earmarked for HHS assistance to refugees and entrants, and most of the rest for progressive programs.
  • Fuel economy: CAFE standards for 2031 were sharply lowered, from a targeted fleet-wide average of 50.4 mpg to 34.9 mpg.
  • Title IX: The Department of Education rescinded the Biden-era extension of protection to LGBTQ students and indicated it would bar trans girls and women from varsity sports. Asked how many of the 242k participants in NCAA women's championship-level sports are trans, NCAA President Charlie Baker thought fewer than 10.
  • School vouchers: Treasury released proposed rules for a voucher program to start in January, in essence transferring federal funding from public to private schools.
Foreign Policy and the Military

Foreign Policy.

  • Pentagon policy chief Elbridge Colby is coordinating a NATO meeting in Poland next month to discuss NATO's future. England, France and the Baltic countries have not been invited. Putin must be delighted.
  • Under intense U.S. pressure, G7 countries agreed to immediately release diesel reserves as the midterms loom.
  • The administration is redirecting $52MM in foreign military aid from Slovakia, North Macedonia, Tunisia and Iraq to Panama, Peru, Ecuador and Colombia, all of which have right-wing governments.
  • After negotiations with Iran at the U.N. broke down, Marco Rubio ordered their delegation to leave the U.S. immediately.

The Military.

  • In a "State of the Force" speech, Pete Hegseth announced a 20% reduction in the number of generals and admirals. Sen. Jack Reed (D-RI) responded, "Reshaping the senior leadership of the world's most powerful military is precisely the kind of decision that requires consultation with and justification to Congress."
  • In the same speech, Hegseth called for a military of "Just warriors," rejecting by slur those he deemed unfit by appearance or identity.
  • The USS Theodore Roosevelt carrier strike group is being redeployed to the Middle East, joining the USS George Washington and USS George H.W. Bush groups already there. The U.S. has eleven carrier strike groups in all.
Immigration
  • Supreme Court: The Court overturned a lower-court ruling that barred deporting immigrants to third countries with which they have no connection. It will hear oral arguments in December and rule definitively later. At a minimum, the Court has facilitated the continuation of this abhorrent program for several months.
  • Quieter, still aggressive: An immigrant advocate said of DHS, "Now they are moving more quietly," but still striving for "gone in ten seconds" outcomes. ICE has signed agreements for assistance from 2,500 state and local law enforcement agencies, is holding mass hearings in immigration courts before recently appointed "deportation judges," and subjects no-shows, often caused by notices sent to wrong addresses, to immediate deportation.
  • Renee Good: Her partner Becca Good and her brother Brent Ganger, on behalf of her large family, are suing the government and certain White House, ICE and DHS staff for her wrongful death.
  • Political cracks: Sen. Jim Justice (R-WV) commented that "ICE raids are too disruptive."
  • New guidance: ICE guidance now bans high-speed car chases and limits vehicle stops to officers with specific training for the activity.
