Week ending August 9, 2026
Hyperbole

Webster definition of hyperbole: language that describes something as better or worse than it really is. Trump is the leading practitioner of hyperbole, perhaps ever, which intermixes with his mendaciousness, delusionalism, and ignorance.

Here are a few examples from Trump's pronouncements this week: On Sunday, Trump said about the planned attacks on Iran he had just called off "It was going to be a massive attack...that would have been, by far, the biggest attack since World War II". Reality: We are running very low on munitions; even if we conduct such an attack, the Iranians would not be deterred. Russia's invasion of Ukraine currently holds that "distinction".

On Monday, he wrote on Truth Social "My REAL Polling Numbers, not those made up by the Fake News Media, are the best they have ever been". Reality: Trump's polling numbers are mostly in the low to mid-30% range and are at or below their all-time low in the wake of 1/06/21.

On Wednesday, Trump said on Fox News "The Strait is going to be open very soon or they are going to get hit very hard...we're having very good discussions". Reality: Such threats have been ineffectual and currently Iran is negotiating with Oman rather than the U.S..

On Thursday, Trump posted "(The) U.S. has massive amounts of munitions". Reality: We apparently only have 20% of our THAAD interceptors and 33% of our Patriot missiles left per the Center for Strategic and International Studies. A U.S. official confirmed that supplies of both missiles were "extremely low" as is our inventory of ATACM and PRSM offensive missiles.

On Friday, he wrote on Truth Social "(The) Ballroom, which is being built ahead of schedule and under budget, is a Gift From President Trump and Great Patriots...There will be no expense to the American taxpayer". Reality: The cost of the Ballroom has soared, it is running behind schedule and at least half of the escalating cost is being paid directly from government funds; to the extent there are private funds involved, the benefits to the donors from our government are likely to far exceed their contributions.

Hyperbole is not confined to Trump and his minions. Here are a few other examples: Bernie Sanders said of El-Sayed's win over Haley Stevens by a scant 1%: "Abdul's victory was, in my view, , the most extraordinary victory that I can think of in modern American politics". Reality: In reverse chronological order, to cite a few other surprise winners, Donald Trump, Doug Jones, Barack Obama, Lisa Murkowski (as a write-in candidate), Thomas Eagleton and Harry Truman all say hello.

Anthony Zurcher of BBC said: "El-Sayed's victory sends tremors through Democratic Party" and Fareed Zakaria wrote "Democrats are facing a dilemma and Michigan's Senate primary perfectly illustrates it...The left has the passion, but the middle still has the votes Democrats need to win in general elections". Reality: Zurcher is being overly dramatic and while Zakaria's view has merit, the RealClear Polling average has Rogers ahead in MI by only 0.3% and other polls did not find Haley Stevens to fare much better than that. Polymarket actually gives El-Sayed a 61% chance to win. He is a superior campaigner to Stevens and may catch on with the electorate and bring in younger, more progressive voters who otherwise may not have voted. Before the MI primaries, the few victories DSA or near-DSA candidates achieved were in urban or university areas. To the extent that DSA-type candidates are not ideal for the Democrats, the Republicans are fielding many candidates who are at least as far removed from the political mainstream or are ethically/morally impaired.

In NBCNews, Kristen Welker referred to the Democrats as a "fractured party". Reality: At least in MI, the losers in the primary and state party coalesced around El-Sayed immediately. We should also bear in mind that the Democrats are a big tent and diverse party compared to the my-way-or-the highway Republicans who march in goose-step to Trump's orders. If the Democrats are fractured, one could argue that the Republicans are irretrievably broken.

Todd Blanche Confirmation

Early Saturday morning, Blanche was confirmed by a 50-49 near-party-line Senate vote with only Collins and Murkowski breaking from their party, as promised, to vote against him. Blanche's written confirmation last Sunday that the $1.8B anti-weaponization fund was dead, unsigned by Trump, and his confirmation that the tax audit immunity the settlement granted was only retrospective in its scope, as had been clear from the outset, was sufficient to allay the qualms of Senators Cornyn and Tillis whose votes advanced Blanche's confirmation vote to the full Senate. (The fund could still be reactivated by Congress, unlikely, or reinstituted directly or indirectly by the DOJ or elsewhere in the administration). That left the count at 49-49 with Senator Cassidy, who had been successfully primaried out by Trump, having the decisive vote. On Friday, Dr. Cassidy—whose support sealed RFK Jr.'s confirmation and also voted to confirm Tulsi Gabbard, Pete Hegseth and Kash Patel—announced that he would vote for Blanche's confirmation. Jen Rubin wrote in response an article entitled "Bill Cassidy Still Can't Find His Spine". Looking back at the entire process, Andrew Tanenbaum wrote "If JFK wrote Profiles in Courage today, it would be a pamphlet. Or maybe a leaflet".

We should bear in mind that Blanche's confirmation was mostly symbolic. Had he not been confirmed, he could have continued as Acting AG until late October and then could stay on after that for as long as a nomination for another AG candidate was pending. On Saturday morning, the Senate adjourned for its annual 5-week summer recess.

Primary Results and Emerging Midterm Strategy

Primary Results: In MI, Abdul El-Sayed defeated liberal Representative Haley Stevens by a scant 1.0% in the Democratic senatorial primary, ultra-progressive and erstwhile DSA member William Lawrence won a 3-way race with 44% of the vote in the battleground MI-07 against two mainstream opponents who equally divided the other 56%, and DSA member Donavan McKinney defeated liberal incumbent Shri Thanedar with a 4% margin in deep-Blue MI-13. On the Republican side, in MI-08, Thomas Smith, who had suspended his campaign, defeated Trump-endorsed Amir Hassan by a striking 17%.

El-Sayed quickly moved to soften his image, saying "Jewish people and Judaism are a light unto the world", "I'm not a socialist, but I'm a scientist", and "I'm interested in solutions to problems". All of this said, AIPAC is considering providing financial support for Republican candidate Mike Rogers. On Friday, the MI Democratic establishment (including recent transplant Pete Buttigieg) and his defeated rivals enthusiastically coalesced at the One Michigan rally kicking off the general election campaign.

More moderate Democrats prevailed comfortably in three other notable House races as incumbent William Bell defeated former Rep. and progressive firebrand Cori Bush by 22% in MO-01 after only beating her by 5% in 2024, moderate incumbent Ed Case, who voted for the initial version of the SAVE Act, beat a progressive challenger by 24% in HI-01, and Blue Dog incumbent Marie Gluesenkamp Perez defeated her nearest Democratic challenger by 20% in the WA-03 jungle primary. Such wins against progressive activists have hardly attracted any attention compared to DSA-adjacent wins in House primaries this year.

In TN, Trump-endorsed incumbent Andy Ogles lost by 6% to former SC Agriculture Commissioner Charlie Hatcher by 6% in the TN-05 Republican primary. Among Ogle's MAGA-adjacent quotes have been "Muslims don't belong in American society" and "Homosexuality has no place in America".

In anti-democratic ballot questions in two Red states, in KS, Amendment 1, which would have introduced elections for State Supreme Court justices, was defeated 63-37%, and in MO, Amendment 4, which would require a majority in each of the 8 CDs to pass a citizen-initiated constitutional amendment, was defeated by 80-20%, and Amendment 5, which would have eliminated state income taxes and replaced them with sales taxes, was defeated by 83-17%.

Election Strategy: Going into this week's primaries, DSA-adjacent candidates had won about 30% of races in districts where Kamala Harris got at least 67% of the vote in 2024, but did not fare well in Battleground or Red districts. The MI results were their first breakthrough in a Battleground.

Here's what some pundits had to say about the state of play: Andrew Tanenbaum, reflecting upon the thoughts Nate Silver shared in the wee hours after the MI election wrote "Progressives are clearly on the move, but not everywhere and for everyone...It works only in limited circumstances. It is not an export product". He went on to add "One of the reasons Democrats appear to be moving to the left is that moderate Democrats are leaving the party and becoming independents".

With regard to the Democratic electorate, former Obama advisor Dan Pfeiffer opined "They want to pick a winner; they just don't trust Democratic politicians to tell them who can really win".

Jen Rubin opined "The latest media 'take'---that Democrats are moving far left--- defies reality. The party has always...established itself as the broadest pro-democracy coalition since the New Deal".

Conor Lamb, who flipped the Republican seat in PA-17 in 2018, wrote "In 2018, the theory of success was 'Elect good public servants who can make our institutions work again'...(now it's) 'We want people who fight. We want people who never back down'. They're uncompromising".

An article in The Economist points out the "paradox" that the 120k highly educated, urbanized and not infrequently gay/queer DSA members are "a group that claims to represent the working class (but) looks and speaks nothing like it".

In the author's view, as a big tent party, the Democrats have always had to harmonize disparate factions within the party, but this is especially challenging today as the general electorate has become increasingly disaffected and social media is so impactful. In this regard, the MI party appears to be off to a good start in embracing its diverse candidates. Meanwhile, seminarian James Talarico in TX and megachurch pastor David Hamilton, who won the KS Democratic senatorial primary, are the kind of candidates who may be able to attract religious voters who have often been reluctant to vote for Democratic candidates.

Meanwhile, Republican candidates need to overcome their association with the increasingly unhinged Trump's low approval ratings which reflect antipathy toward his war of choice with Iran and his failure to address affordability concerns and deliver the favorable economy he promised during his campaign. In the latter regard, the average Freddie Mac mortgage rate was an ominous 6.66% earlier this week, the year-over-year CPI was 3.5% as of June, and economic growth shrunk to 1.5% in the second quarter. With regard to the Iran War, the impasse with an increasingly antagonistic Iran continues and the prospects for returning to the status quo ante for the Strait of Hormuz are not promising.

To be sure, the Democrats have been saddled with Graham Platner's abusive behavior toward women which caused him to drop out of the Maine Senate race with much notoriety. However, in comparison to Former WH staffer and current OH Rep. Max Miller, who allegedly threw scalding water at his wife, OH Senator Bernie Moreno's daughter, and held a gun to her head, as well as breaking his 2-year-old daughter's collar bone and withholding her beloved stuffed bunny during a custody exchange, Platner is a choir boy. Moreover, Miller is not alone in the Republican caucus as SC Rep. James Edwards has just been censured by the House Ethics Committee for his behavior toward young female staffers, and TX Senate candidate Ken Paxton spent our 250th anniversary with his mistress in Iceland.

Looking at the data, G. Elliott Morris, the curator of the Strength in Numbers substack, shared that his model currently gives the Democrats an 85% chance to win the House and a (optimistic) 55% chance to take control of the Senate. Linking this to the national popular vote, Morris' model indicates that the Democrats need to win it by 4% to take the House and 7% to take the Senate. His former colleague Nate Silver has the Democrats currently leading the generic congressional ballot by 6.4% and RealClear Polling has them ahead by 7.0%.

Making such extreme stipulations indicates that Iran feels that it has the upper hand and is in no hurry to resolve the conflict.
