The Kennedy Center: Trump's Obsession and the Destruction of a National Memorial

This week's news featured Trump's obsession with having his name imprinted on the Kennedy Center. Before launching his campaign to accomplish this goal, he would have been well served to reflect on these words from Pericles: "What you leave behind is not what is engraved in stone monuments, but what is woven into the lives of others."

This effort of self-glorification began early in his second term when Trump dismissed the Center's board, replaced them with handpicked loyalists, and appointed himself chairman. At that time he said "We don't need woke at the Kennedy Center, and some of the shows were terrible. They were a disgrace that they were even put on. So I'll be there until such time that it is running right." In immediate response to Trump's actions, advance ticket sales dropped by 50%, a plethora of performers cancelled their appearances, the Washington National Opera ended its 50-year residency, and as a consequence FY 26 revenue for the Center was down 60% from FY24 and the National Symphony Orchestra announced its departure for the following season.

As the Center was struggling, in December the board voted to affix Trump's name above Kennedy's on the facade of the Center. With his main goal accomplished, in February Trump announced that the Center would be closed in July for two years of repairs. Doing so without congressional approval was clearly illegal, and in May U.S. District Judge Christopher Cooper blocked the closing and ordered that Trump's name be removed. Presumably this was done, but the facade was covered with a tarp and scaffolding to obscure the name(s). Then in August, the board voted to inscribe "Restored and Renovated by President Donald J. Trump" directly below the Center's name, and this week Judge Cooper again blocked installing Trump's name on the Center and its grounds. In response, the board voted to immediately close the Center for renovations which Trump said would only happen if his name is allowed to be put on the building, notwithstanding the $257 MM of congressionally approved funding for this purpose. When last seen, Trump was photographed on Air Force One holding a rendering which read "Kennedy Center DEMOLISHED."

This lamentable episode reminds one of the bust-out scheme in Goodfellas where the mafia buys an interest in the Bamboo Lounge, drains its credit, sells off its contents and then burns it down. George Conway opined "To destroy things he can't possess or control is his essential nature." And the WAPO's culture critic Philip Kenicott aptly summed things up: "No other episode in Trump's second term better encapsulates the triviality of his agenda and the pettiness of the man...The ambition is always personal while the damage is global."

As would be expected, the Kennedy family is outraged. Joseph Kennedy III wrote that Trump "seems to be focused on trying to appropriate the legacy rather than serving the people that he was elected to serve. That, ultimately, is going to be a legacy that he's going to have to wrestle with." Jack Schlossberg added "JFK's legacy is unbreakable: the moon, the Civil Rights Act, Cuban Missile Crisis, and the arts. Trump is a broken brand in a nose-dive decline." Kerry Kennedy characterized the Center's closure as "hostage taking by the president."

We should note that the Kennedy Center is the only memorial to JFK in Washington D.C. Meanwhile, Trump is consumed with several building projects, including the White House Ballroom, Arc de Trump, Reflecting Pool, East Potomac Golf Course, and Lafayette Park renovation, as well as eponymous branding such as the Trump $1 coin, his photo on passports, his signature on currency, banners with his likeness draped over the DOJ and DOI, Trump Institute for Peace, Trump Accounts, Trump-class Battleships, Trump Rx, etc.

Election News: Mail-in Ballots and the Integrity of Elections

On Monday, the Supreme Court declined to allow the USPS to determine whom to send mail-in ballots to by cross-referencing federally-curated state citizenship lists, thereby upholding U.S. District Judge for MA Indira Talwani's TRO blocking implementation of the new rules written to comply with an EO Trump signed in March. The unsigned one paragraph order, apparently approved by a 7-2 margin, stated that the government's appeal was "unlikely to succeed on its merits". Justice Alito wrote a dissent on the decision co-signed by Clarence Thomas. The two of them are the only justices who sided with Trump on all four notable decisions which went against him this term: restricting the right to levy tariffs, leaving birthright citizenship intact, denying the right to fire Federal Reserve Board Governor Lisa Cook, and this mail-in decision. Not only did the 24 Blue state litigants and the League of Women Voters prevail, but seven Republican-led states had filed a brief asking the Supreme Court to prevent the new mail-in rules from being implemented this year.

As would have been expected, Trump railed against the decision, writing "The Court's inability and unwillingness to do the right thing for our Country will go down, in a very negative way, in the annals of History. This Supreme Court is bullied and cajoled by the Radical Left into making decisions that have set America back at least a hundred years" (Ed. note: generally Trump is receptive to reversion to 19th century norms). With regard to his three Court appointees, he added "These are not the people I interviewed to serve on the United States Supreme Court, they are merely a shell of their original selves".

CO Secretary of State Jena Griswold optimistically proclaimed "Trump's attempt to sabotage the 2026 election has failed". However, we should all be mindful of what George Zornick wrote in The Guardian: "The extraordinary danger of this moment is that a president who believes that he is the law, and the law is him, remains intent on disrupting the electoral process". After all, there are 45 more days until the election, plenty of time for other voter suppression, election day intimidation and disruption is likely, and as we learned in 2020, the 2-month period after the election is rife with peril and this time Trump's appointees will impose no guardrails.
