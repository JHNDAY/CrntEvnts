Reuters: 41 of 62 Competitive Races Show Distancing
A Reuters review found Republicans in two-thirds of 62 competitive House and Senate races broke with Trump on policy or played down ties to him. Twelve campaigns removed his name, image or endorsement from their websites. Iran was the biggest flashpoint: a dozen Republicans voted to curb his war powers or called for an end to the fighting. This matches the anonymous senator George quoted.

The White House Strategy: "Put Him on the Ballot"
NPR reported that Trump is campaigning more than he did at the same point in 2018, despite worse approval numbers than the year Democrats gained 40 House seats. Chief of staff Susie Wiles said the aim is to turn out low-propensity voters who show up for Trump himself.
NPR (Jul 22)
The Numbers Behind the Distance
Reuters/Ipsos put Trump's approval at 32%, and registered voters favored Democrats 44% to 37%. An Emerson poll had Democrats up 11 on the generic ballot. The Guardian listed seven Senate toss-ups: Alaska, Iowa, Maine, Michigan, New Hampshire, Ohio and Texas. Cook moved Kansas to Toss Up.

In The Free Press, Montefiore argued that the war pitting Israel and America against Iran and its proxies opens a new chapter, with China and Russia quietly backing Tehran. His central question is who will rule the region, a contest he sees as larger than Israel's own dilemmas.

The Core Case
Rattner's October 2 New York Times piece, "Six Charts That Show Just How Much We Need A.I.," argues that only higher output per worker raises living standards, that productivity growth has lagged since the early 1970s, and that AI is the most promising fix. He calls job-loss fears "all valid worries" but manageable.
NYT Opinion (Oct 2)
Keynes by the Numbers
Keynes predicted a 15-hour week by about 2030. Rattner calculates Americans need only about seven hours of work to match 1930 living standards, yet average 38.3 hours, down just 20% since 1930. We took the gains mostly as income, which frames the time-or-money debate the group had.
NYT Opinion
Real-World Gains He Cites
Siemens' AI co-pilot cut unplanned machine maintenance time by 25%. A Kaiser Permanente note-taking tool saved nearly 16,000 hours of physician documentation, relevant to David's interest in AI in medicine. John Deere's camera sprayers saved 31 million gallons of herbicide in 2025.
NYT Opinion
The Fuller Policy List
Beyond retraining, wage insurance and securing Social Security and Medicare, Rattner proposes government testing of AI products, possible kill switches, cooperation with China on AI security, a "token tax" on AI usage, ending data-center tax exemptions and raising the corporate rate back toward 35%.
NYT Opinion

Fortune: Efficiency Goes to Output
Dan Schawbel argued in Fortune that AI-freed time tends to be filled with more work, the pattern Larry described with computer drafting. Jamie Dimon and Bill Gates have predicted shorter weeks, but IWG's Mark Dixon said, "Everyone is focused on productivity, so no time soon."
Fortune (Aug 21)
Billable Hours Are Rising, Not Falling
Bloomberg Law reported that demand for lawyers' time rose 4.2% in the first half of 2026, against normal growth of about 1.5%, per Citi. Two-thirds of firms say associates use AI daily, and three-quarters expect it to affect the billable-hour model by 2028. Sally's "10 cases instead of three" looks closer to what is happening.
Bloomberg Law (Sep 3)
"Weakening, Not Dead"
About 7 in 10 firms have adopted AI tools since 2022, but billing has been slower to change, with more talk of fixed fees and subscriptions. ABA Formal Opinion 512 bars billing clients for time spent learning AI tools without their request.
Virginia Lawyers Weekly (Aug 10)
Who Trains the Next Lawyers?
Georgetown's Jonah Perlin warned that as AI absorbs research once done by junior associates, new lawyers lose the work that trained them: "Students are not ready for that." It is a reminder that research hours were also apprenticeship hours.

Arizona as the Test Case
Axios called Arizona a test case for AI's energy and water crunch: utilities must double in four to five years what took over a century to build. Arizona paused some data-center tax incentives, and Ohio and Texas moved to curb theirs. Google's Phoenix-area site uses air cooling, which saves water but uses more electricity.
Axios (Jun 18)
Larry's Water Worry, With Nuance
Arizona has 100 to 115 data centers running and about 86 more planned. ASU water-policy director Sarah Porter said newer chips and cooling methods use far less water, and that data centers alone are unlikely to destabilize the state's supply if local oversight stays strong.
AZFamily (Sep 23)
Labor's Share Hits a New Low
S&P Global reported that the share of U.S. economic growth going to workers' pay and benefits fell to 54.1%, a record low, and that AI adoption could push it further. That is the trend in Rattner's chart, and George's call for median figures would show it even more sharply.
S&P Global (Jun 30)
An AI CEO Floats Universal Income
The "far-out" idea John mentioned got a mainstream airing in June when Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei proposed that, if AI permanently reduces demand for labor, governments fund a universal basic income by taxing AI companies or raising capital-gains taxes. He also backed wage insurance and incentives against replacing workers.
