Looking Ahead: How to "Unwind the Mess"

Source: Afterwords (AfterwordSep3025)

The question as recorded: assuming Democrats win Congress in the 2026 midterms and the presidency in 2028, how should they use that power to restore democratic norms and repair damage from the Trump administration?

Challenges identified

  • The party lacks a coherent unifying philosophy beyond opposing Trump
  • Internal divisions between progressive and centrist factions
  • Supreme Court composition limits policy options for years
  • Trump's changes were carried out with long-term strategic planning
  • Damage may take generations to repair
  • Need to avoid repeating past mistakes
"It came from a plan that had been obviously thought through over a long period of time, and putting in place folks like Supreme Court justices to enable it. The question is, what do we start to do? How do we start to get things back to a better world that we would like?"Discussion participant

The group agreed to continue, asking members to consider: "You have the magic wand, what do you do with it?" The framing was meant to steer toward positive policy goals rather than criticism.

Oct 62025Off-cycle session · 48 min

Between the Lines: the first Magic Wand deep dive

Source: Political Discourse Meeting Summary (PDOct0625) · John Day, Alan Nemeth, "Ed Murrow" AI avatar

1 · Historical "Deals"

Square Deal (T. Roosevelt), New Deal (FDR, a 60-year coalition), Fair Deal (Truman), and John's editorial "Bum Deal" for Project 2025's attempt to dismantle the New Deal. Alan countered that Lincoln also pursued progressive economics (railroad, land-grant universities).

2 · Political realignment

Drawing on Daron Acemoglu (2024 Nobel): the 1920s coalition (Democrats as the party of manual labor) flipped after technology created an "education premium" and automation devalued manual work.

3 · Working-class liberalism

Democrats should stop assuming everyone shares the same aspirations and avoid an elitist face. Alan observed that conservatives operate in a zero-sum mindset, which liberalism rejects.

4 · Government guardrails

John: "I take away from Trump's success that we've overdone it." The task is to streamline process while keeping essential safeguards and codifying norms into law. Alan proposed two axes: operational (how) and goal-oriented (what).

"We have to get rid of the idea that everybody in the country has the same aspirations that we do... That's one of the fundamental ways that the Democrats keep losing elections that they should really win."John Day

Ed Murrow avatar

A ChatGPT voice-mode avatar, loaded with Acemoglu's lecture and a Project 2025 summary, served as a "reference librarian." Alan asked it for historical evidence on reducing guardrails; it discussed post-Watergate reforms and, when pushed, searched for articles on legislative entrenchment.

Follow-up items recorded

  • Research Project 2029
  • Examine post-Watergate reforms and their erosion
  • Explore codifying norms without excess bureaucracy
  • Reframe the challenge around the 2026 midterms rather than 2028 (suggested by Alan's wife)
  • Elections noted: Spanberger (Virginia) and Sherrill (New Jersey) governor races
"If we're lucky, as Ben Franklin told us so many years ago, we'll be able to keep our democracy, but... at the moment, it's definitely being challenged... to the very roots."John Day
Oct2025Portal card · reading packet

The Magic Wand Challenge readings

Sources: GovPolicyDeals · PoliticalRealignment · LiberalismBlueprint · Project2029

Evolution of Government Relationship to Citizenry

Traces precursors from Lincoln to McKinley (Homestead Act, Interstate Commerce Act, Sherman Antitrust Act, civil service reform), then rates "government support level" by era: Square Deal 70%, New Deal 95%, Fair Deal 75%, "Bum Deal" (2025+) 25%. Charts the counter-movement from the American Liberty League through Reagan, Gingrich, the Tea Party and Project 2025. Cumulative impact listed: union membership 35% (1950s) to 10% (2020s); wealth inequality highest since the 1920s.

Lesson drawn: "Transformative change rarely happens overnight."

The Great Political Realignment

Acemoglu's causal chain: technology → economic impact → social divergence → political realignment. College-educated share of the population rose from 10% (1940) to 50% today; 85%+ say "politicians don't speak for people like me." What working-class voters want, per the piece: minimum wage increases, stronger unions, job protections, job training, trade protection, and respect for community values, rather than tax-and-redistribute or cultural focus.

Remaking Liberalism

Liberalism's three promises: sharing, public services, and voice. An eight-part blueprint for "working-class liberalism": return to community, focus on job creation, pro-worker technology, self-government, cultural economics, business leadership, targeted regulation, and policy realignment. Closing line: "It takes an idea to beat an idea."

Project 2029 (NYT, Shane Goldmacher, Jun 30, 2025)

A plan for a Democratic policy agenda modeled on Project 2025, released in quarterly installments through Democracy: A Journal of Ideas. Advisory board includes Jake Sullivan, Neera Tanden, Anne-Marie Slaughter, Justin Wolfers, Felicia Wong and Jim Kessler. Central debate: is the party's problem its ideas or its messaging? Two competing visions: the "abundance" agenda (Ezra Klein, Derek Thompson) and the populist wing (Bernie Sanders). Project 2025 polled 15% support vs. 63% opposed (NYT/Siena, Sept 2024).

"The oldest truism in politics is you can't beat something with nothing."Andrei Cherny, Project 2029 organizer
Oct 142025Regular session · 83 min

The Magic Wand Challenge comes to the full group

Source: Session summary (CEOct14) · John Day, Alan Nemeth, Geoffrey & Hilary Kent, Sally Fleschner, Larry, Beth Davis, Francesca Nannetti

"If Democrats won everything you can imagine in the elections, what do they do with it? How do we unwind the mess Trump has created?"Alan Nemeth, introducing the challenge

Strategic framework

  • Set aside how Democrats get power; focus on what they would do with it
  • Learn from Trump's actions: identify real system faults he exposed (permitting had gotten out of hand, though his methods are not the answer)
  • Rebuild old institutions or reimagine their missions? (Department of Education as example)

Project 2029

  • Centrist response to Project 2025
  • Jake Sullivan, Neera Tanden among leaders
  • Progressive critique: "not ambitious enough"

Healthcare

  • "Medicare for All" carries baggage; Geoffrey proposed "Medical Care for All"
  • Separate the care system from the payment system
  • Alan: "Healthcare is a human right," a possible point of Republican agreement

Common ground

The Pete Buttigieg approach: find bipartisan wins such as removing phones from schools.

Guns

Pick winnable battles. The previous assault-weapons ban did not cut overall gun murders but did reduce mass shootings; most gun deaths are suicides.

Working-class liberalism

  • Acemoglu's proposal discussed; cultural mistakes cited: the "deplorables" comment, transgender issues, undervaluing trades and farming
  • Josh Shapiro dropped the bachelor's degree requirement for Pennsylvania state jobs on his first day
  • Alan on Norway and Finland: about half of graduates go academic, half vocational, "both viewed as equal status in the society"

Shutdown strategy

Hilary asked: "How long will it go on, and should the Democrats cave?" Alan: "Democrats are starting to win the argument," as premium bills arrive (a Massachusetts plan went from $0 to $59/month). The group's poll: hold firm, "for a while."

Group agreement points

  • Cultural understanding: not everyone shares Northeast liberal values
  • Simple messaging: the Bill Clinton model, direct impact on people's lives
  • Unity: cross-aisle agreement has power
  • Overreach: Trump will overplay his hand
"Progressive government faces a fundamental problem: many important issues are long-term, but Democrats must secure votes with immediate, tangible benefits."John Day, core message (paraphrased in summary)

Francesca: "If there's unity, you can accomplish a lot." Beth: "When you find unity, we should advertise it."

James Talarico enters the conversation

Source: Session summary (CEOct28Smmry) · John Day, Hal, George Model, Sally Fleschner, Geoffrey Kent, Francesca Nannetti, Beth Davis

Who he is (as recorded)

  • 36 years old, Texas Senate candidate
  • Subject of a favorable NYT profile about a week earlier
  • Presbyterian seminarian, Austin Presbyterian Theological Seminary
  • Raised in hardship by a single parent; experienced homelessness as a child

Platform and style

  • Working-class focus
  • Balanced immigration approach
  • Reclaiming faith and patriotism from Republicans
  • Skilled on TikTok and social media; draws large crowds
"The only reason I'm a part of the Democratic Party is because I believe it is the best vehicle to organize working people to win political power and make their lives better. And if it's not doing those things, then it needs to change."James Talarico, quoted in session

On religion: faith-based but not a "Bible thumper"; denounced white Christian nationalism as "bad for the country, bad for Christians"; no preference for one religion over another.

John's assessment

A new voice for progressives, well read in philosophy, speaking from a working-class liberalism perspective, with possible appeal to right-leaning Texas voters.

"I think the progressive movement has to disabuse itself of the idea that everybody wants to be like us... Some people want to work on farms, some people want to lay cable, and it's important to get their vote in order to avoid this minority rule that we're suffering."John Day
  • Francesca: young Democrats are moving in this working-class direction (Sanders, AOC, Talarico)
  • John: Democrats governed with pro-worker policies but failed at communication; recalled FDR building a coalition that included the South
  • George: how does Talarico compare to Mamdani? Talarico hews to the center; Mamdani is a democratic socialist
  • John, on the NYC race: "We're in a period of transition for the democratic and progressive movement"; don't stay with the familiar just because it is familiar

The summary's key-themes section lists "James Tallarico represents model of 'working class liberalism'" under Democratic messaging problems. The session also discussed "day-after" planning (Francesca: "There doesn't seem to be the day-after plan"), a theme that applies to the wand itself.

Part II · Connecting the threads to November 2026

What the group discussed in 2025, set beside what happened. Election figures are network projections and the AP's Texas race call as of Nov 4–5, 2026; counts continue.

1 · The wand was meant for 2029. Half of it arrived in 2026.

Then

Sep 30: "assuming Democrats win Congress in 2026 and the presidency in 2028." Oct 6: Alan's wife suggested reframing the challenge around the 2026 midterms rather than 2028.

Now

Democrats won both chambers in a wave election. CBS reports more than 40 House flips; NBC reports Senate flips in Kansas, Iowa, Texas, Ohio, Maine, North Carolina and South Carolina; CNBC reports Republicans flipped no seats. Trump remains president until January 2029.

The 2026 reframe proved the timely one. The group's question now splits in two: what a Democratic Congress can do facing a presidential veto (block, confirm or reject nominees, investigate, fund or defund), and what a full wand would do after 2028.

2 · Talarico: from a New York Times profile to the U.S. Senate

Then

Oct 28: John called him a new voice speaking from working-class liberalism, with possible appeal to right-leaning Texans. Jan 2026: the primary framed as a test between mobilizing the base and widening the appeal.

Now

Talarico won the Mar 3 primary over Crockett (CNN projection). Ken Paxton beat Sen. John Cornyn in the May 26 GOP runoff. On Nov 3 the AP called the race for Talarico, the first Democrat elected to the Senate from Texas since 1988 and the first to win any statewide race there since 1994.

Correction to the Oct 28 record: the summary describes Talarico as running "against Ted Cruz." The 2026 seat was Cornyn's; Cruz is not up until 2030. The summaries also spell the name "Tallarico"; the correct spelling is Talarico.

3 · Working-class liberalism, tested at the ballot box

Then

Acemoglu's blueprint, the Shapiro degree example, and John's "everybody wants to be like us" argument. The readings listed what working-class voters say they want: wages, unions, job protections, training.

Now

CBS exit data: 89% of voters called cost of living a major or crucial factor. In Texas, Democrats also defeated Gov. Greg Abbott and won the Texas House for the first time in more than two decades (CNBC).

The results are consistent with the group's view that economic, kitchen-table messaging matters. They do not by themselves settle the Crockett-versus-Talarico strategy debate: a national wave with a 38% presidential approval rating (CBS) lifted candidates of many styles.

4 · Healthcare: from shutdown premiums to a governing lever

Then

Oct 14: the group polled to hold firm on ACA premium subsidies; Alan said Democrats were "starting to win the argument"; healthcare as a "human right" as possible common ground.

Now

NBC reports that with both chambers, Democrats gain leverage to restore enhanced ACA subsidies and reverse Medicaid cuts. Any bill still needs the president's signature or a veto-proof margin.

5 · "He will overplay his hand"

Then

Oct 14 agreement point: Trump will overplay his hand and face political consequences. Oct 28: Republicans such as Hawley and Greene breaking ranks when constituents are hurt.

Now

Coverage widely describes the result as a backlash against the president (Variety, NBC). CBS puts his approval at 38%, historically low for a midterm.

6 · The questions still open

  • Plan, not just win. The Sep 30 worry was that Republicans won with decades of planning. Project 2029's quarterly papers are the closest Democratic equivalent; whether they shape the new Congress is unknown.
  • Guardrails. John's Oct 6 question remains: how to codify norms so future officials cannot ignore them, without adding "so much gunk in the machine that it can't operate"?
  • Rebuild or reimagine. Alan's Oct 14 example: restore the Department of Education as it was, or rethink its mission?
  • The day after. Francesca's Oct 28 point about missing day-after plans now applies to Democrats themselves.
  • The Mamdani question. Hal and George worried in Oct 2025 that Republicans would use Mamdani against Democrats nationally. He won the NYC mayoralty in Nov 2025, and Democrats still swept in 2026.
