Political Strategy · Acemoglu's Call for Democratic Big Tent Coalition
Beyond Populism: Building Shared Prosperity Through Inclusive Governance
John Day84:42–89:26
I saw an important Acemoglu's OpEd in the NYT this morning. He's advocating for a liberalism that is defined and supported by working people—people who do manual labor. His core argument: why populism matters is that these working-class voters have been left behind in economic growth. People with college degrees have benefited enormously from the economy's shift toward technical expertise. So it's critical to bring these people back into the fold.

The problem is that a lot of working-class voters felt ignored by Biden, even though he passed legislation that would have helped them directly. They didn't see it, so they voted for Trump. The risk of current populism is that if you embrace adversarial framing—everything as oppressor versus oppressed—you alienate the middle class and prevent coalition building.

Acemoglu's solution: Democrats need a big tent. Include traditional liberal progressives alongside working-class voters, united by a shared prosperity agenda. Historically, FDR achieved this in the 1930s-40s with his New Deal Brain Trust, the labor movement, and conservative Southern Democrats—strange bedfellows unified by shared prosperity.

Biden tried this, made honest effort, and did make progress. But ultimately he failed, and that's led to four years of distress. The article argues: governance requires feasible policy, not just slogans. And progressives are right that we need new ideas on inequality, opportunity, and corporate excess. But it can't be only one side—that won't work. You need both pragmatic policy AND progressive vision. That's a pretty good assessment.
"Democrats need a big tent that includes both traditional liberal progressives and working-class voters—united by a shared prosperity agenda, not divided by slogans." John Day, citing Acemoglu's OpEd in the NYT
Key Principles for Big Tent Coalition
  • Working-class voters left behind by economic growth; college-educated workers benefited from technical shift
  • Biden's legislation existed but failed in messaging and implementation—voters didn't see benefits
  • Adversarial populism alienates middle-class; prevents broad coalition
  • FDR model: unified New Deal, labor, Southern Democrats through shared prosperity, not ideology alone
  • Conservatives right about: governance needs feasible policy, not slogans
  • Progressives right about: need ideas on inequality, opportunity, corporate limits
  • Success requires BOTH pragmatic implementation AND progressive vision
  • Listening to voters and including them beats top-down dictates
