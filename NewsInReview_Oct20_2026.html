News in Review
Week ending October 20, 2026 · Deep Dive

This week's news featured Trump's obsession with having his name imprinted on the Kennedy Center. Before launching his campaign to accomplish this goal, he would have been well served to reflect on these words from Pericles: "What you leave behind is not what is engraved in stone monuments, but what is woven into the lives of others."

✦ ✦ ✦
The Kennedy Center Spectacle

This effort of self-glorification began early in his second term when Trump dismissed the Center's board, replaced them with handpicked loyalists, and appointed himself chairman. At that time he said: "We don't need woke at the Kennedy Center, and...some of the shows were terrible. They were a disgrace that they were even put on. So I'll be there until such time that it is running right."

In immediate response to Trump's actions, advance ticket sales dropped by 50%, a plethora of performers cancelled their appearances, the Washington National Opera ended its 50-year residency, and as a consequence FY 26 revenue for the Center was down 60% from FY24 and the National Symphony Orchestra announced its departure for the following season.

As the Center was struggling, in December the board voted to affix Trump's name above Kennedy's on the facade of the Center. With his main goal accomplished, in February Trump announced that the Center would be closed in July for two years of repairs. Doing so without congressional approval was clearly illegal, and in May U.S. District Judge Christopher Cooper blocked the closing and ordered that Trump's name be removed. Presumably this was done, but the facade was covered with a tarp and scaffolding to obscure the name(s).

Then in August, the board voted to inscribe "Restored and Renovated by President Donald J. Trump" directly below the Center's name, and this week Judge Cooper again blocked installing Trump's name on the Center and its grounds. In response, the board voted to immediately close the Center for renovations which Trump said would only happen if his name is allowed to be put on the building, notwithstanding the $257 MM of congressionally approved funding for this purpose. When last seen, Trump was photographed on Air Force One holding a rendering which read "Kennedy Center DEMOLISHED."

This lamentable episode reminds one of the bust-out scheme in Goodfellas where the mafia buys an interest in the Bamboo Lounge, drains its credit, sells off its contents and then burns it down. In a similar vein, George Conway opined: "To destroy things he (Trump) can't possess or control is his essential nature." And the WAPO's culture critic Philip Kenicott aptly summed things up: "No other episode in Trump's second term better encapsulates the triviality of his agenda and the pettiness of the man...The ambition is always personal while the damage is global."

As would be expected, the Kennedy family is outraged. Joseph Kennedy III wrote that Trump "seems to be focused on trying to appropriate the legacy rather than serving the people that he was elected to serve. That, ultimately, is going to be a legacy that he's going to have to wrestle with." Jack Schlossberg added: "JFK's legacy is unbreakable: the moon, the Civil Rights Act, Cuban Missile Crisis, and the arts. Trump is a broken brand in a nose-dive decline." Kerry Kennedy characterized the Center's closure as "hostage taking by the president."

We should note that the Kennedy Center is the only memorial to JFK in Washington D.C. Meanwhile, Trump is consumed with several building projects, including the White House Ballroom, Arc de Trump, Reflecting Pool, East Potomac Golf Course, and Lafayette Park renovation, as well as eponymous branding such as the Trump $1 coin, his photo on passports, his signature on currency, banners with his likeness draped over the DOJ and DOI, Trump Institute for Peace, Trump Accounts, Trump-class Battleships, Trump Rx, etc.

A Thumbnail Comparison: Kennedy and Trump

Given that Trump is so focused on appropriating JFK's legacy, a quick comparison reveals the gulf between them.

Messaging at Inauguration: JFK inspired the country to do great things with "Ask not what your country can do for you—ask what you can do for your country," while Trump sowed division: "The American carnage stops right here, right now."

Administration's Style: JFK embodied Camelot; Trump embodies Lifestyles of the Rich and Famous.

Military Service: JFK served heroically on PT-109. Trump obtained draft deferments for bone spurs and called avoiding venereal disease "his personal Vietnam."

Support for the Arts: JFK invited Robert Frost to recite poetry at his inauguration with 50 other artists attending; featured top ballet and opera companies and leading classical musicians at the White House. Trump favors UFC events, Duck Dynasty, Kid Rock, Lee Greenwood, and Hulk Hogan.

Spouses: Jackie was viewed as a paragon of style, grace and culture and cofounded the White House Historical Association. Melania is inaccessible, sported an "I Really Don't Care" jacket, and paradoxically launched an anti-bullying campaign.

Staffing: JFK brought in the "best and the brightest"—individuals with extensive government experience and top business leaders. Trump favors those with reality TV and on-air news/opinion experience, real estate backgrounds, and loyal political supporters.

War and Peace: JFK authorized the Bay of Pigs debacle, increased military presence in Vietnam to 16k, negotiated the Laos settlement, resolved the Cuban Missile Crisis, and established the Peace Corps. Trump launched the Iran War, removed Maduro, attacked "drug" boats, brokered the Abraham Accords, and established a Board of Peace.

Civil Rights Stance: JFK declared civil rights a "moral issue," created the EEOC, supported expansion of voting rights, and ensured admission of blacks to southern universities. Trump prefers return to antebellum days, restores monuments to the Confederacy, and decries DEI.

Statements on Germany: JFK declared in 1963, "Ich bin ein Berliner." Trump said in April: "He (Friedrich Merz) doesn't know what he's talking about...No wonder Germany is doing so poorly, both economically and otherwise."

Freedom of the Press: JFK said in 1961: "Without debate, without criticism, no Administration and no country can succeed—and no republic can survive...And that is why our press was protected by the First Amendment." Trump posted Friday on Truth Social that the media "shouldn't be able to constantly write or report FICTION and LIES when they're covering the President," and has now banned CNN, MSNBC and Politico from the White House.

Famous Books: JFK wrote Profiles in Courage with Theodore Sorenson's assistance. Trump had The Art of the Deal written for him by Tony Schwartz.

With apologies to Lloyd Bentsen, "I knew Jack Kennedy. Trump is no Jack Kennedy."

Election News: Mail-in Ballots and Voter Suppression

On Monday, the Supreme Court declined to allow the USPS to determine whom to send mail-in ballots to by cross-referencing federally-curated state citizenship lists, thereby upholding U.S. District Judge for MA Indira Talwani's temporary restraining order blocking implementation of the new rules written to comply with an executive order Trump signed in March. The unsigned one-paragraph order, apparently approved by a 7-2 margin, stated that the government's appeal was "unlikely to succeed on its merits."

Justice Alito wrote a dissent co-signed by Clarence Thomas. The two of them are the only justices who sided with Trump on all four notable decisions which went against him this term: restricting the right to levy tariffs, leaving birthright citizenship intact, denying the right to fire Federal Reserve Board Governor Lisa Cook, and this mail-in decision. Not only did the 24 Blue state litigants and the League of Women Voters prevail, but seven Republican-led states had filed a brief asking the Supreme Court to prevent the new mail-in rules from being implemented this year.

As would have been expected, Trump railed against the decision, writing: "The Court's inability and unwillingness to do the right thing for our Country will go down, in a very negative way, in the annals of History. This Supreme Court is bullied and cajoled by the Radical Left into making decisions that have set America back at least a hundred years." With regard to his three Court appointees, he added: "These are not the people I interviewed to serve on the United States Supreme Court, they are merely a shell of their original selves."

Colorado Secretary of State Jena Griswold optimistically proclaimed: "Trump's attempt to sabotage the 2026 election has failed." However, we should all be mindful of what George Zornick wrote in The Guardian: "The extraordinary danger of this moment is that a president who believes that he is the law, and the law is him, remains intent on disrupting the electoral process." After all, there are fewer than three weeks until the election, plenty of time for other voter suppression, election day intimidation and disruption is likely, and the period immediately following the election remains rife with peril.

Current Polling and Senate Races

The NYT/Siena poll results released this week continue to reflect dissatisfaction with the performance of Trump and his administration. Overall approval stands at 38% with 59% disapproving. On key issues: Economy (39/60), Tariffs (39/58), Cost of Living (28/71), Immigration (43/55), and the Iran War (31/66).

When asked which party's congressional candidate they planned to vote for, respondents favored the Democratic candidate by 8%, consistent with the Silver Bulletin's 7.5% and RealClear Polling's 8.6%.

Notwithstanding his unfavorable approval rates, Trump has made the midterms about himself, asking voters to "pretend that I'm running" and announcing that he plans to campaign in 35 congressional battlegrounds. He also said this week that "We have the greatest economy in history." Thomas Massie (R-KY), much reviled by Trump, commented: "This was Donald Trump's election to lose, and he's doing everything he can to lose it."

Turning to the Senate elections, Cook, Sabato and general polling split the incoming Senate 47-46 in favor of the Republicans with 7 Senate races which could go either way. The competitive races show Democratic leads, though often within the margin of error: Michigan (+2.1%), New Hampshire (+4.8%), Ohio (+4.8%), Maine (+1.8%), and Alaska (+1.7%). In order to take control of the Senate, the Democrats will need to win five of the seven races, or possibly only four if Lisa Murkowski decides to caucus with the Democrats, which she has raised as a possibility if the Senate splits at 50-50.

Corruption and Abuse of Power

Trump made 28,700 stock trades during the first 17 months of his second term—more than the 22,000 combined trades for all 535 members of Congress. Russian oligarch Umar Kremlev, a close associate of Putin, paid hundreds of thousands of dollars for the after-party in The Bahamas for Trump Jr.'s wedding in May. Yesterday Trump claimed that his son had paid Kremlev back for his "gift."

Frank Bruni commented: "As a snake inherently slithers, a Trump instinctively grifts." After the 35% reduction in tax audits last year, the administration delayed the release of the annual IRS report on unpaid taxes. Joe diGenova, who was leading the "grand conspiracy" probe into Biden and Obama officials who investigated Trump, abruptly resigned from the DOJ after a meeting with Todd Blanche where he was badgered for not proceeding more rapidly.

The DOJ is appealing to the 4th Circuit as it attempts to revive vindictive prosecutions against James Comey and Letitia James. The Interior Department plans to install 12 signs outside the Smithsonian's National Museum of American History because its leaders "have altered the museum's role from accurately and coherently telling America's story to using the museum as a tool to advance personal, radical ideologies."

The Iran War's Mounting Toll

Continuing his attempt to defeat Iran with verbiage, Trump said: "The failing nation of Iran wants to make a deal quickly and badly." However, the reality is grimmer. The Congressional Budget Office reported that the Iran war drove 40% of inflation in Q2 2026 and that it will add 0.5% to inflation in Q1 2027.

For the third time, the House voted to end the Iran War with a 220-204 vote, with 7 Republicans voting with all Democrats. The Pentagon's Inspector General reported that Iranian attacks on U.S. bases around the Arab Gulf have caused $3.7 billion of damage. A frustrated service member said: "We're not defending these bases. We're just watching them get destroyed."
