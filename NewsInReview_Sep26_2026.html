This week Trump was busy satisfying his authoritarian cravings. Three activities stood out before we turn to the midterms and a few other items.

Assault on the Press

Trump banned CNN, MS NOW and Politico from the White House, posting that outlets "shouldn't be able to constantly write or report fiction and lies," then vowed an "assault on FAKE NEWS." U.N. Speech and the Xi Summit

Sasha Abramsky called it "Hitler updated for the digital age"; Senator Andy Kim said Trump "never misses a chance to humiliate himself in front of the world."

Primary retrospectives show mainstream Democrats winning roughly half their primaries in the 43 most competitive districts, versus 18% for progressives and 20% for DSA-aligned candidates—prompting Third Way's Matt Bennett to argue a House or Senate majority "will be because moderates flip seats." Trump's own endorsement win rate topped 97%, though barely one in ten campaign ads even mentions him.

News in Review

Week ending September 26, 2026
The Authoritarian Ledger

As the president's physical health, cognition and connection with reality all continue to decline, he attacks and bans the press, lashes out at his perceived enemies, obsesses over vanity projects to commemorate his own name, antagonizes our long-time allies, threatens to annihilate Iran, and conspires to steal the midterm elections. Meanwhile, just over the last ten days, his Project 2025 implants and their minions have removed 760k enrollees—4% of the total—from ACA coverage, politicized NIH grants, attacked and mistreated immigrants to achieve deportation quotas, and are in the process of rewriting American history at the Smithsonian and restoring the Confederate Memorial for its return to Arlington National Cemetery next year.

This week Trump was busy satisfying his authoritarian cravings. Let's discuss his three prime activities in this regard before we move on to an update on the midterm elections and a few other items.

Assault on News Organizations

Last Friday, Trump banned CNN, MS NOW and Politico from the White House, posting "Media outlets shouldn't be able to constantly write or report fiction and lies when they're covering the President of the United States, the Trump Administration or the United States of America. Other news outlets to follow." Then on Monday, he added that the White House "is initiating an assault on FAKE NEWS, something that has grown like Cancer—No matter how GREAT my achievements, they trivialize and demean." That same day, ABC, CBS, Fox News and NBC—the four other networks which along with CNN share TV pool coverage on a rotating basis—announced that they would not provide pool coverage until CNN is reinstated.

Meanwhile on the legal front, the three banned media outlets sued Trump in Washington D.C. federal court early on Monday to reverse the ban. On Thursday, U.S. District Judge Timothy Kelly, a first-term Trump nominee, issued a 14-day TRO restoring their access to the White House citing "constitutional harm" and "the importance of the free press." The administration did not immediately comply with the TRO, causing the outlets' attorneys to request a non-compliance hearing. In response, the DOJ cited national security concerns, but Judge Kelly was unimpressed and left the TRO in place, and the White House then reluctantly complied on Friday morning. However, on Friday night the White House reneged, blocking CNN from flying on Air Force One with Trump to attend the Tennessee/Texas football game. Trump's ongoing attempts to silence unfavorable news coverage evoke the Nazi's anti-free press law, which called for news organizations to "regulate their work in accordance with National Socialism as a philosophy of life."

Speech at the U.N.

On Tuesday, Trump had yet another opportunity to indulge his authoritarian impulses when he gave a nearly 40-minute address to the U.N. General Assembly. The speech was alternately bellicose, self-congratulatory and strikingly unilateral.

On the bellicosity front, he said that he was deciding whether or not to "annihilate the Islamic republic," promised that Cuba's government would fall, referenced "to the victor belong the spoils," and said "If necessary, we will use our military might to secure the vital national interests of the United States."

Regarding self-congratulation, Trump referenced the kidnapping of Maduro and seizing Venezuelan oil, extolled the recent Greenland deal (which changed very little), bragged about killing suspected drug traffickers at sea, claimed that he personally ended eight wars, cited U.S. leadership in AI—calling it Super Intelligence—and claimed he won reelection "in a giant landslide."

It was striking that he made no references to collective action while addressing an organization whose charter was built around collectivism. Quite to the contrary, he characterized the International Criminal Court as a "rogue institution," railed against "any attempt to construct a globalist scheme to control" AI/SI, espoused a "might makes right" doctrine, and made no calls for collaboration of any kind.

Reflecting on the speech, Sasha Abramsky wrote in The Nation: "This was, quite simply, Hitler updated for the digital age. I challenge you to find one sentence in that monstrosity of a U.N. speech that the German Führer would have wanted to distance himself from." Senator Andy Kim opined, "Trump never misses a chance to humiliate himself in front of the world."

Summit With Xi Jinping

Trump broke precedent by meeting Xi at Joint Base Andrews as he alighted from his plane, the first time a president met a foreign leader there since 1962. Reflecting on his comportment during the summit, Yun Sun, Director of the China Program at the Stimson Center, commented that "recognizing Xi as his peer, and recognizing China as the United States' peer, is creating a window of opportunity for China to further build up its strength to erode and to undermine U.S. supremacy."

Other than extending the U.S./China trade truce into January and agreeing to speak on the side during the forthcoming Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation and G20 meetings later this year, it appears the summit had little substance. This gave Trump an opportunity to put on a reality TV performance, his favorite genre, touting his "truly great friendship with Xi" (Ed. note: does Trump have any real friends other than Natalie Harp?) and saying "We've made great strides, very positive for both countries." The highlight he envisioned was the made-for-TV lavish state dinner attended by key cabinet members, leading tech titans and his family—which, ironically, was not televised by the five major networks because of Trump's petulant ban on CNN, MS NOW and Politico, which had not yet been resolved. He invited all the dinner guests to view the shell of the nearby Ballroom and bragged that the new granite White House helipad could land "any helicopter in the world," adding that Xi "is an expert on stone aside from many other things and loves good granite," and beforehand made a point of showing Xi the autopen replacing Biden's image in the Presidential hall of fame. Shawn McCreesh observed in the NYT that Trump "never seems more pleased than when he is hosting wealthy and powerful people at his table." His showmanship was in stark contrast to the demeanor of the unexpressive and businesslike Xi, who was well-prepared for the meeting and most notably said, "China hopes the U.S. side will adhere to the correct position of opposing 'Taiwan independence,' and handle the Taiwan question with prudence to lay a solid foundation for bilateral strategic cooperation and the development of China-U.S. ties."

Midterm Elections: The SAVE Database and a Map Blocked

With the three liberal justices in dissent, the Supreme Court issued an unsigned ruling on Friday allowing states to use the government's SAVE database, which includes Social Security numbers and other personal information, to help determine voter eligibility. The majority wrote that the 90-day "quiet period" under federal law—which prohibits wide-scale changes to voter rolls during that time frame—"limits the potential impact" of their order, and in her dissent Justice Jackson agreed the impact would be quite limited in 2026, although not afterward.

This said, after Trump cited a DHS finding that 15.9k non-citizens were registered to vote in Nevada in his July nationwide address, the state said last month that the actual number of potential registered non-citizens was only 185, and after conducting a case-by-case review just announced this week, that all 185 of them were indeed citizens. Similarly in Texas, after the Secretary of State's office flagged 2,700 voters as potential non-citizens, this week it sent only 117 cases to AG Ken Paxton's office for investigation. It is clear from these two case studies that the SAVE database is flawed and/or incomplete and is not yet suitable, if ever, for use in managing state voter rolls. Moreover, the charges of extensive non-citizen voting nationwide are evidently Trumped-up, as even the Heritage Foundation has documented fewer than 100 such cases since 2000.

In a small victory for the Democrats, on Friday the Court blocked the use of the newly gerrymandered Missouri congressional map, very likely preserving the seat of Democrat Emanuel Cleaver.

Primaries Retrospective

Articles by Chris Stein in The Guardian and Ed Kilgore in RealClearPolitics set forth some interesting information. Seventy-four House members—42 Republicans and 32 Democrats—did not run for reelection. Twenty-eight of them ran for other, generally higher, offices, with 15 of those being defeated in primaries. In addition, 11 House members (6 Democrats and 5 Republicans) lost their primaries, as did Republican Senators Cassidy and Cornyn.

Although 4 of the losing Democrats lost to DSA-tied candidates, a Brookings study found that in the 43 most competitive districts for November, 34 mainstream Democrats, 8 progressives and only 1 DSA member (Donavan McKinney in MI-13) prevailed in their primaries. In terms of primary success rate, about 50% of mainstream Democrats, 18% of progressives, and 20% of DSA members won (the remaining 20-plus percent of primaries presumably pitted candidates within the same categories against each other).

On the Senate side, the only DSA-adjacent candidates who prevailed over mainstream opponents were Abdul El-Sayed in Michigan and Angie Nixon in Florida.

Matt Bennett of Third Way opined, "If Democrats are to win the majority in the House and/or the Senate, it will be because moderates flip seats from red to blue." Although Trump-endorsed gubernatorial candidates lost 5 primaries, none of his endorsees lost in Senate races and only 4 lost in 229 House primaries. Taken together, Trump's endorsement win rate was nearly 97% per a USA Today analysis. This said, a CNN survey of 500 TV ads in the first half of this month found that only 12 of them mentioned Trump. Although he said he planned to campaign in 35 competitive races, it was just announced he would instead be campaigning in deep-Red Alabama and Oklahoma.

Miscellaneous Notes on Elections

RealClear Politics suggests that the -0.5% change in Net Disposable Income between Q3 2025 and Q2 2026 may be the key metric for the midterms. This has only happened four other times since 1950 in midterm years, and the party out of power gained between 9 seats (in 2022) and 48 House seats (in 1958 and 1974). The Republicans have reserved $682 million of ads so far spanning September 21 through Election Day versus $491 million for the Democrats. Retiring Republican Congresswoman Nancy Mace commented, "This midterm is going to be a f...king bloodbath."

Although Susan Collins has reduced Troy Jackson's polling average lead to 1.0% and led him by 3% in the latest NYT/Siena poll, ProPublica has disclosed that her campaign received an illicit $150k contribution in 2019 orchestrated by the then-CEO of defense contractor Navatek, Martin Kao. Notes and Quotes on Selected Other News: Immigration

DHS has spent $410 million so far to induce 31 third countries to accept deportees.
