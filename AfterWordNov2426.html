01 NVIDIA: Resignation, Rebuff, Reinstatement in Eleven Days High coverage ▼
Huang Resigns over Two Affairs, One with a Suspected Spy
Huang stepped down as CEO on November 12 after reports of two extramarital affairs. One involved Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed executive whom U.S. officials suspect of spying for China; Beijing denies she is a spy. The other reportedly involved an NVIDIA executive.
The Next WebNPR
Trump: "He Learned a Big Lesson"
Leaving for Asia on November 16, Trump told reporters he would not mind seeing Huang reinstated. AP framed it as a split inside the administration: the day before, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent had called for a wholesale cleanup at NVIDIA while investigators looked into whether banned chips reached China.
EuronewsAP
The Board Says No; Congress and DOJ Move In
On its November 17 earnings call, NVIDIA's chairman said the board had unanimously accepted the resignation and did not intend to reinstate Huang, even as quarterly sales doubled to a record $115.8 billion. Huang agreed to transcribed interviews with three House committees, the Justice Department opened a criminal probe of Megaspeed, and Commerce closed a remote-access loophole for advanced chips.
NPRAPBoston Globe
November 23: The Board Reverses Itself
Six days later, NVIDIA's board unanimously reinstated Huang as president, CEO and director. Its SEC filing says the board received input from senior levels of the U.S. government and, on that basis, is confident his return poses no national security risk. That detail bears directly on the group's debate over whether Trump pressured the company.

02 The Midterms: Largest Win for Either Party in 50 Years High coverage ▼
A 14-Point House Margin, the Biggest Since Watergate
NPR's final tally had about 56% of House votes going to Democrats and 42% to Republicans, a spread no party has matched since 1974. Newsweek put the Democratic share at 56.1%, above the 2018 wave's 53.4%. George's "over 14%" figure matches.
NPRNewsweek
A Record Raw-Vote Lead and 260 Seats
NBC News reported Democrats' House vote lead topping 8.8 million, breaking the 1974 midterm record of 8.7 million. If Democrats hold every race they lead, they will have 260 seats, the most since the 1991–93 Congress.
NBC NewsNPR
What Drove It: Trump and the Economy
ABC News Voter Polls found that seven in ten House voters think the economic system favors the wealthy, and they backed Democrats about three to one. Voters who identify with neither party, about a third of the electorate, went Democratic 58% to 38%.
ABC News
Mississippi Elects Scott Colom
After a week of counting, Colom unseated Cindy Hyde-Smith, becoming Mississippi's first Black senator since Reconstruction and its first Democratic senator in four decades. Coverage widely noted the turnabout John raised two weeks ago: in 2023, Hyde-Smith blocked his nomination to the federal bench.
APNewsOneMississippi Free Press

03 The Three Amigos in Shenzhen: A Win for Xi, a Photo for Trump High coverage ▼
First-Ever Three-Way Summit, Mostly Xi's Show
Trump, Xi and Putin met for about an hour and 20 minutes at a lakeside villa on the sidelines of APEC on November 19. AP judged the event largely a victory for Xi, who used it to showcase China as a convener of global powers while both rivals eye a future without Trump.
APAFP
The Image Was the Deliverable
NPR concluded the summit's main product was arguably the picture itself: an American president gathered with the leaders of China and Russia for the first time in history. Critics worried the three would carve the world into spheres of influence, close to George's "regional hegemons" reading.
NPRNPR
What Came Out: UN Reform, Fuel, Nuclear Talk
China and Russia said the three agreed on UN reforms, boosting fuel supplies and dialogue on nuclear arms, which tracks closely with George's list of outcomes. Xi urged the powers to avoid conflict and keep energy markets stable. Arms-control advocates said far more than diplomatic theater was needed.
APSCMP
Schumer: "Like a Cheap Violin"
Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer said Xi and Putin were playing Trump "like a cheap violin." A second three-way meeting is possible if Putin attends the G20 at Trump's Doral resort in December.
AFPAP

04 The Ground Stop and the Patch Wave: Outages Without Hackers High coverage ▼
A Historic Nationwide Ground Stop
The FAA halted departures nationwide on November 13 with about 3,500 flights in the air, citing intermittent telecommunications outages; thousands of flights were canceled or delayed. Duffy blamed old hardware, and Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said there was no sign of an attack.
Fox WeatherFox 5 NY
The Cause: A Wiring Upgrade That Cascaded
Duffy later explained that a contractor replaced copper wire with fiber at a radio site in El Dorado, Arkansas; when the router came back on, a problem surfaced at a radar site in Amarillo and cascaded into the shutdown. That is the "slapdash" wiring change John described. The administration is pressing Congress for $30 billion more in upgrades.
AP via Spotlight PAAP Explainer
Six Outages in Six Days, Blamed on Patches
The FAA, United, Apple, Microsoft, major banks and even the NSA's classified network went down in the same stretch. Reuters reported experts pointing to rushed software fixes as AI finds flaws faster than systems can be tested, and officials told Congress there was no coordinated cyberattack.
ReutersSiliconANGLEEntrepreneur
Microsoft's 1,400 Flaws, Up from 170
The Wall Street Journal reported that Microsoft's October update fixed about 1,400 security flaws, a record, compared with roughly 170 a year earlier. That is the 1,400-bug figure on John's slide. Security writers argued the fear of AI-found flaws, more than any attack, drove the self-inflicted damage.

05 Serbia: The Students Beat Vučić Sustained coverage ▼
"The Students' List Has Won"
Vučić conceded the October 25 parliamentary election, projecting 135 to 140 seats for the student-backed list against about 105 for his party in the 250-seat parliament. He blamed independent media, unnamed European countries and an "American deep state" for the loss.
AP via CTVPBS NewsHour
An Upset the Polls Missed
An Ipsos poll published days before the vote had Vučić's party ahead 47% to 40%. The movement began after the November 2024 canopy collapse at Novi Sad's train station killed 16 people and set off nearly two years of anti-corruption protests.
Reuters via Kyiv PostAP via Ahram
Experts, Not Students, on the Ballot

06 The Power of Balance: The Times Editorial and Its Critics Sustained coverage ▼
The Board's Thesis
The New York Times editorial board's November 9 piece, the one John presented, argued that Democrats won in Trump country by running as "economic populists and cultural moderates." It added that many voters outside battleground states may not have noticed how far the party leaned toward social moderation.
NYT Editorial (mirror)
The Board's Own Reservation
The editorial also said the board disagreed with some Democratic messages and regretted that the party had not laid out more of an affirmative agenda for raising living standards. That echoes the group's earlier argument for a written platform.
NYT Editorial (mirror)
Pushback from the Left
Critics on the left read the editorial as turning a repudiation of Trump into a call for moderation and fiscal austerity. A Portside piece noted that even centrist analyst Ruy Teixeira called affordability-focused populism a successful formula this cycle, while warning it could lose force after the election.
WSWSPortside
Brookings: Economics, Not Ideology
A Brookings analysis of the 2026 primaries found the real divide was insurgent versus establishment rather than left versus center. Winners were defined by working-class populism, and Democratic voters backed progressive economics while pulling back on "woke" cultural positions, the same pattern Hilary and Sally described.

08 The Bank of AI: Circular Financing and the Capex Boom Emerging story ▼
Hyperscalers Head Past $1 Trillion
Axios reported that Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Microsoft and Oracle are expected to spend roughly $800 billion this year and over $1 trillion in 2027. Other projections put 2027 above $1.3 trillion, in line with John's slide.
AxiosIEEE ComSoc
NVIDIA as Lender to Its Own Customers
NVIDIA lined up six Wall Street firms to create more than $500 billion in capital pools to finance its customers' projects. UBS analysts said the move raises further questions about circular financing, where suppliers help fund purchases of their own products: George's "bank of AI."
AxiosNPR
Echoes of the Telecom Bust
Fortune noted that in past tech bubbles, revenue round-tripping and companies financing their own customers made the eventual damage worse. Analysts have compared NVIDIA's strategy to the vendor financing that preceded the early-2000s telecom collapse, the kind of correction John expects.
FortuneTomasz Tunguz
Concentration Risk
A Nasdaq analysis found 54% of NVIDIA's revenue tied to just three hyperscaler clients. Acting as a financial backstop for customers secures demand, it said, but becomes fragile if end demand weakens.
Nasdaq
