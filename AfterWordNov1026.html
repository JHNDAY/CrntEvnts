01 The Blue Tsunami: Democrats Take Both Chambers, Seven Senate Flips High coverage ▼
"Relief Is on the Way"
AP and NBC reported that Democrats flipped seven Senate seats (Kansas, Iowa, Texas, Ohio, Maine, North Carolina and South Carolina) for a 54–44 majority with Mississippi and Alaska still open. Schumer called it a generational remake of the Senate map and summed it up in five words: "Relief is on the way." Coverage noted the result may be the largest popular-vote win for either party since 1976.
NBCAP / NYT via Boston 25
Biggest House Majority Since 2008
The Times projected Democrats on pace for their largest House majority since 2008's 257 seats, the same benchmark George cited. Speaker Mike Johnson conceded that holding the House meant defying more than a century of midterm history. Record turnout topped 124 million ballots, passing the 2018 midterm record, per the University of Florida Election Lab.
NYT / APGray DC
Texas and Iowa: Results Few Predicted
The Washington Examiner's takeaways highlighted Gina Hinojosa ousting three-term Gov. Greg Abbott and James Talarico beating Ken Paxton, while Josh Turek's win of more than four points in Iowa pushed Democrats past 50 Senate seats. Both Texans celebrated with a stop at Whataburger.
Washington ExaminerNBC
Statehouses: The Howard Dean Map, Reversed
Stateline reported Democrats flipped at least 12 legislative chambers, the most since 2008, ending Republican dominance of state government that began in 2010. New trifectas came in Michigan, Minnesota, Nevada, Pennsylvania and Wisconsin, where Democrats flipped both chambers for the first time in nearly two decades. NCSL called it a wave that upended GOP control of legislatures.
StatelineNCSLABC
Trump: "IN A LANDSLIDE!" and a VETO Hat
On Truth Social, Trump posted that he wasn't on the ballot and Republicans would otherwise have won in a landslide, the line George quoted. He also posted an image of a red "VETO" hat, attacked Fox News and Gov. Abbott, and told Fox the Iran war had made for an unpleasant election night.
IBTimesTheGrioNBC
Sources: AP NYT NBC NCSL Stateline
02 NVIDIA's Huang, an Alleged Chinese Spy, and Rubio's Unheeded Memo High coverage ▼
Huang Admits the Affair; Board Opens a Probe
In a message to employees on November 8, Jensen Huang acknowledged an affair and apologized to his family and staff; the Washington Post published his letter. NVIDIA's board said independent directors with outside counsel would investigate whether a foreign intelligence operation had targeted him.
TRT WorldBoston Globe
Who Is Alice Huang?
Answering Fran's question: she is a Chinese citizen and senior executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based company that bought NVIDIA's advanced AI chips. U.S. intelligence, based on electronic surveillance, concluded she had been recruited by China's Ministry of State Security.
Boston Globe / NYTGround News
Rubio's Classified Memo, Then Silence
The Times reported that Rubio oversaw a highly classified memo this spring warning senior officials, ahead of Trump's May visit to Beijing, about Huang's ties to her. Hal's account matches the reporting: Trump had been briefed about a year earlier and took no action, and Raw Story summarized that the warnings fell on deaf ears.
NYT (via DNYUZ)Raw StoryTaiwan News
Trump: Huang Is "a Fine Person"
Per Bloomberg, Trump defended Huang on Sunday as a "fine person" who should remain CEO, and told reporters he did not regret his own inaction. NVIDIA pushed back on the Times reporting, saying allegations about Megaspeed had been investigated and debunked last year. Congressional reaction was described as hostile.
Taiwan News / BloombergTweakTownLatin Times
Sources: NYT WaPo Boston Globe Bloomberg
03 The Polls Got the Winner Right and the Size Wrong High coverage ▼
A Bigger Miss Than 2016, in Reverse
Axios reported that surveys correctly anticipated a blue wave but missed its force, underestimating Democrats by roughly 4 points, a gap comparable to the 2020 miss and larger than the average errors of 2016 and 2024.
AxiosPolitical Wire
House Vote: About 9 Projected, About 13 Delivered
Straight Arrow News, like George, put the final polls at about Democrats +9 on the national House vote and the count at about +13. In Texas's governor's race and South Carolina's Senate race, the averages favored Republicans and Democrats won both.
Straight Arrow NewsPew Research
14 of 15 Senate Races Underestimated
AllSides compiled analysis finding Democrats underestimated in 14 of 15 key Senate races, with an average error of 4.4 points. One explanation offered: pollsters scarred by 2016, 2020 and 2024 were willing to miss low on Democrats rather than repeat those errors.
AllSidesNewsweek
Pew: Direction Right, Margin Understated
Pew's first look, drawing on RealClearPolitics, Silver Bulletin and FiftyPlusOne averages, found pre-election polls captured the direction of the election while generally understating Democrats' advantage, in a year Pew had flagged as uniquely difficult to poll.
Pew ResearchRealClearPolling

04 Still Counting: Colom's Lead Nears 3,000; Alaska's Two Sullivans Developing story ▼
Colom's Lead Over Hyde-Smith, Election Week
Each dot is a reported count update; hover for the time. The lead dipped by 38 votes on Nov 9, then rose to 2,990 by late Nov 10 (AP), with about 98% counted. Race not yet called.
Mississippi Free Press liveblogDemocracy DocketMississippi Today / APWLBT
Mississippi: From 65 Votes to Nearly 3,000
Scott Colom's lead over Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith has widened almost every day since Election Day, when it stood at just 65 votes. It reached about 2,500 by Friday and 2,604 on Tuesday afternoon. Late Tuesday, AP put it at 2,990 with about 98% counted, though it still had not called the race. Counties must certify by Friday, November 13, and Colom expects the final Hinds County tally to settle it. Hyde-Smith says she remains confident. Republicans have asked that affidavit ballots be preserved, and Mississippi has no automatic recount.
Mississippi TodayWLBTYahoo NewsDemocracy Docket
The Judgeship That Created a Candidate
John's anecdote is well documented: Hyde-Smith blocked Biden's 2023 nomination of Colom to a federal judgeship, while Sen. Roger Wicker returned his blue slip without objection. Semafor observed that had she not objected, Colom would be on the bench for life and she would likely lack a real opponent. If he wins, he would be Mississippi's first Black senator since Reconstruction.
SemaforElectoral-VoteMississippi Today
Alaska: Ranked-Choice Count on November 18
With 74% counted, Mary Peltola led Sen. Dan Sullivan by fewer than 700 first-choice votes, and neither had 50%. Overseas ballots are accepted for 15 days, with ranked-choice tabulation set for November 18. Both campaigns projected victory. Peltola would be the first Alaska Native in the Senate.
Gray DCAlaska's News SourceAlaska Public Media
The Other Dan Sullivan, and Peltola's Odds
The coverage is less sure than George was. A Petersburg man named Daniel J. Sullivan drew about 3,600 votes, more of whose voters ranked Peltola next. But an Alaska Beacon analysis and a Newsweek estimate found that second choices from the other eliminated candidates could tip the final round narrowly to the senator. Alaskans also voted on repealing ranked-choice voting.
NewsweekAlaska BeaconFairVote

Before the vote, Jeffries had built the House message on an affordability agenda covering healthcare, groceries, gas and housing, and told CNN that impeachment rarely came up with voters.

06 Slingshots and Firsts: Talarico, El-Sayed, and a Record for Women Governors Sustained coverage ▼
Talarico: "You Were My Slingshot"
The speech John played was widely reported. Talarico cast the race against three Goliaths: the richest man, the most powerful man, and the Washington establishment. He credited Texans as his slingshot, called his campaign a politics of love, and closed with Jesus overturning the tables in the Temple. He is the first Texas Democrat elected to the Senate since Lloyd Bentsen in 1988.
KSATBeliefnetIBTimes
The Seminarian Who Translates Scripture
Fox profiles stressed that Talarico is a former middle-school teacher and Presbyterian seminarian who carried Tarrant, Collin and Denton counties, all Trump counties in 2024. The full transcript is available for comparison with Lincoln's Second Inaugural.
Fox 5Full Transcript
El-Sayed: "Call Me Senator"
The line Sally and George quoted was reported verbatim: El-Sayed told Trump that after JD Vance swears him in, he can just call him senator, and he called Vance's constitutional duty to swear him in a "delicious irony." He will be the first Muslim U.S. senator. National Review called the speech defiant, offering Republicans no olive branch.
Michigan AdvanceComplexNational Review

Seventeen women will serve as governor in 2027, beating the record of 14, according to Rutgers' Center for American Women and Politics. Keisha Lance Bottoms becomes Georgia's first Black and first woman governor, Deb Haaland the first Native American governor, and Peggy Flanagan the first Native American woman senator.

On women in Congress the press disagrees, as George and Larry did. ABC cited CAWP projecting 139 women, down from 152, while Newsweek projected at least 155, a new record. Axios reported a record for Democratic women in the House. Expect the figure to settle as late races are called.

07 Court Reform: Hearings Now, Laws Later Emerging story ▼
SCOTUSblog: Power to Debate, Not to Pass
SCOTUSblog assessed that the new Congress can ensure reform proposals, from ethics rules to term limits to expansion, get a hearing, but the filibuster and Trump's veto put the ambitious ones out of reach for now. It also tracked the rumors about Alito's retirement, which George raised.
SCOTUSblogAlterNet
Term Limits: The Popular Option
Fran's preference has broad support. A Marquette Law poll found 73% of Republicans, 71% of independents and 87% of Democrats favor Supreme Court term limits, and expansion draws far more resistance. Sen. Sheldon Whitehouse's 2026 bill would set 18-year terms with an appointment every two years.
Washington TimesLas Vegas SunPuck
Narrowing the Docket, as George Suggested
Stateline noted that, beyond expansion and term limits, reformers have discussed narrowing the kinds of cases the Court can consider. Renewed pressure followed the Court's April ruling weakening Voting Rights Act protections for majority-Black districts.
StatelineCBS News
The Comparative Case Sally Made
Reform advocates echo Sally's point about other democracies: every democracy younger than ours has term or age limits for its top court, and 49 of 50 states use term limits, age limits or retention elections for their highest courts.
StatelineBloomberg Law
Sources: SCOTUSblog CBS Stateline Puck
08 Abroad: Israel's Arab Kingmakers and the ANC's Worst Night Developing story ▼
Israel: Record 19 Arab Seats, No Majority
Michael's 19 seats match the reporting. Ra'am and the Joint List won a record total amid turnout reported near 80% in the Arab community. Ynet's partial results put Netanyahu's bloc at 54, seven short of 61, while Eisenkot's bloc plus the Arab parties reached 66.
YnetAhram OnlineJewish Insider
Eisenkot's Arab-Party Dilemma
Al Jazeera reported the Arab parties have offered to back Eisenkot, but he and other opposition leaders had ruled out such a coalition. Their conditions included accepting Israel as a Jewish state, backing conscription and denouncing Hamas. Yair Golan was the first opposition leader to call for the alliance. Protesters have gathered outside Eisenkot's home on both sides of the question.
Al JazeeraAl-MonitorSCMP
Netanyahu Cries "Stolen"
Supporting Sally's bet that he will survive somehow, AP reported Netanyahu and Likud amplified unsubstantiated claims that Arab-led parties "stole" the election, while Israeli officials said the results stand. The new Knesset was due to be sworn in on November 10, the day of our session.
AP via Arab NewsAl Jazeera
South Africa: The ANC at 31%
Michael's figure is confirmed. With 99% counted, the ANC took 31% in the November 4 municipal elections, its worst result ever, down from 45.6% in 2021, amid anger over water and power outages. It lost outright control of more than a hundred municipalities. Zuma's MK party placed third, and Ramaphosa called the decline concerning.
