The Metaphor: Where Vision Fails and Succeeds

During recovery from cataract surgery, one confronts the nature of obscured vision. The Trump administration's approach to governance demonstrates a parallel condition—cataracts of a figurative kind. In some areas, the administration's vision is so clouded as to approach blindness. In others, they see with crystalline clarity.

Where Vision is Clouded: Our post-WWII strength rested on alliances. NATO's Article 5 was invoked only once—after 9/11—through Canadian solidarity spanning two centuries. The foolishly abandoned JCPOA had contained Iran through diplomacy and sanctions; now we wage war against a predictably defiant adversary willing to choke the Strait of Hormuz and strike our Gulf allies. USAID, at 0.5% of the federal budget, generated soft power returns exceeding defense spending; its elimination will cost hundreds of thousands of preventable deaths. Ukraine's moment of vulnerability for Russia has passed; our reduced commitment squanders European security. Immigration crackdowns devastate agricultural, meatpacking, healthcare, and hospitality sectors amid a sharply declining birth rate. Restrictions on graduate programs, research funding, and foreign students erode our greatest competitive advantage. Clean energy curbs and environmental rollbacks degrade both nation and planet. Tariffs and war compound the domestic affordability crisis.

Where Vision is Crystal Clear: The administration pursues self-enrichment with alacrity—Trump, his family, businesses, and insiders profit handsomely, including from the Iran War. Catering to the ultra-wealthy and petrochemical conglomerates drives tax policy and deregulation. Exercising power to accomplish goals and exact revenge is relentlessly pursued. Project 2025's downsizing aims to restore a robber baron era—an age the Triangle Shirtwaist Factory and Jim Crow made infamous.

Tariffs: Legal Flexibility and Economic Consequences

Early this week, Trump imposed 50% tariffs under Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930 on 500 Canadian products (accounting for roughly $20B in annual U.S. imports), claiming discrimination against American goods. Notably, 85 products on the list are not even produced in Canada. On Friday, as the 10% global tariff imposed 150 days prior expired, Trump imposed 10%/12.5% tariffs on 80 countries representing over 99% of U.S. imports.

The prior tariffs utilized Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974, which includes a time limit. The new tariffs invoke Section 301, pertaining to countries burdening U.S. commerce in "unjustifiable," "unreasonable," or "discriminatory" ways. Critically, Section 301 imposes no time limit if the tariffs withstand legal challenge. Ana Swanson of the New York Times observed: "Over the past 17 months, the Trump administration has cycled through trade law after trade law." The administration appears committed to tariff permanence.

The Iran War: Escalation and Willful Blindness

As the war continues with no end in sight, the administration intensifies its hyperbole. Trump announced our military is "locked and loaded" for a "massive attack" on Iran, "bigger than ever before." Secretary Rubio responded to Iran's foreign minister's invocation of "an eye for an eye" by asserting: "The president's policy is a head for an eye." Both apparently overlooked Gandhi's admonition that "An eye for an eye will only make the whole world blind"—which technically applies to Rubio's formulation as well.

Iran's Houthi proxies in Yemen have closed the Bab-el-Mandeb Strait at the southern end of the Red Sea to Saudi-related shipping and have struck two tankers. Some have suggested the Iran War was partly a distraction from the Epstein files release; now the administration considers strikes against Al-Qaeda-affiliated JNIM militants in Mali to distract from the Iran War itself. Perhaps Cuba awaits.

Secretary Hegseth appeared before the Senate Appropriations Committee seeking $67B supplemental funding. He stated current war spending has reached $37.5B—likely a significant underestimate, as independent analysts suggest the true cost approaches $100B. When pressed for details, Hegseth proved evasive: we are "covertly" guiding ships through the Strait; China and Russia's involvement on Iran's behalf operates at "different levels"; our capacity to strike nuclear facilities under Pickaxe Mountain remains classified. Senator Gary Peters (D-MI) declared: "We are in the midst of another forever war. You, sir, are the failure." Senator Kirsten Gillibrand (D-NY) noted the contradiction: "You said we have 'obliterated' Iran's ability to create nuclear weapons, yet eight months later in April 2026, the president launched an active war claiming Iran was within two weeks of having a weapon."

Later in the week, both chambers conducted symbolic votes to end the war. The House resolution passed 214-208, with four Republicans—Tom Barrett (MI), Warren Davidson (OH), Brian Fitzpatrick (PA), and Thomas Massie (KY)—joining all voting Democrats. The Senate resolution failed 47-49, with Susan Collins voting yes and John Fetterman voting no (cancelling each other), while Katie Britt, Mitch McConnell, Lisa Murkowski, and Rand Paul abstained. Of 518 combined votes across both chambers, only six members voted against their party's dictated position.

Immigration Enforcement: Systemic Cruelty and Dysfunction

The administration is considering proposals that green card applicants at foreign consulates seeking permanent residence post a $100k bond. More troublingly, over 100,000 people have received letters from DHS demanding $998 for each day they remained in the U.S. after immigration judges ruled they had no legal right to stay. Though enabled by 1998 legislation, such fines had never been assessed before.

Following the January Minnesota ICE shootings, Acting ICE Director David Venturella promised all agents would have body cameras by July's end. Despite $20MM allocated for this purpose, agents involved in the Houston and Biddeford shootings remained unequipped. Border Czar Tom Homan told reporters this week that ICE was "training the trainers" and hoped for deployment "as soon as possible."

A Guardian study finds only one of 26 "vehicular attack" cases DHS has pursued resulted in conviction. Fifteen of the 26 accused never faced criminal charges; eight cases were dismissed; one person pleaded guilty to "destruction of government property"; one case remains pending. The Office of Refugee Resettlement is compiling information on 800 Afghan immigrants "who may be in vulnerable situations," potentially enabling immigration enforcement. An ICE spokesperson claimed the agency "evaluates all available information for every applicant," yet former ICE Director Sarah Saldana said of apparent Biddeford shooter David Brouillette: "That person should be nowhere near dealing with the public."

Election News: Midterm Signals and Party Calculations

On Tuesday, Trump incongruously stated: "The Midterms will have no impact on me. I'm just here to do the right thing."

Maine Senate Race: Troy Jackson secured the Maine Democratic senatorial nomination in Bangor this weekend. Unlike his predecessor, Jackson is a fifth-generation logger from Northern Maine—a genuine man of the people. Notably, Susan Collins, Jackson's opponent, also hails from Northern Maine with sixth-generation logger parents. Three preliminary polls showed Jackson ahead by 5%, with a third showing Collins leading by 1%.

Other Developments: In Texas, the Paxton vs. Talarico race appeared a dead heat, though Paxton must be concerned having agreed to debate his more polished rival. Arizona saw election-denier Andy Biggs win the Republican gubernatorial primary with a 60% margin over David Schweikert, now leading Democrat Katie Hobbs by 6% in preliminary polling. Election-denier Alexander Kolodin won the Arizona Secretary of State Republican primary by 20% and faces Democrat Adrian Fontes. In Arizona's AZ-01 swing district, former NFL kicker and MAGA devotee Jay Feely will challenge progressive Democrat Amish Shah. In AZ-04, Democrat Greg Stanton defeated a progressive challenger and will face McCain Republican physician Zudhi Jasser. In AZ-06, moderate Republican Juan Ciscomani will run against moderate Democrat Joanna Mendoza, a former Marines drill sergeant whose slogan is "Battle Tested."

New Jersey Voting Error: Governor Mikie Sherrill disclosed that a Motor Vehicle data system error resulted in 6,600 self-identified non-citizens becoming eligible to vote between June 2023 and June 2024. Analysis suggests fewer than 400 actually voted. While unlikely to have altered statewide results, Trump and his allies will exploit this.

Congressional Retirements: Sixty-two House members (37 Republicans, 25 Democrats) are not seeking reelection—the second-highest number since 1930 and likely to surpass 1992's 65 retirements.

Election Forecasts: The New York Times polled six election experts on House control. All projected Democratic gains of 5 to 11 seats, with Nate Silver projecting a 226-209 Democratic margin. On Senate control, one expert predicted 51-49 Democratic; three projected 50-50; two expected 51-49 Republican. A 50-50 split could see Lisa Murkowski become an Independent caucusing with Democrats, granting them control.

2028 Primary Schedule: The DNC Rules and Bylaws Committee voted for a pre-Super Tuesday schedule: South Carolina first, followed by Nevada, New Hampshire, New Mexico, Michigan, and Virginia. The full DNC will vote in August, possibly altering this order.
