The United States was built on a federalist principle: communities retain authority over their values and priorities, while federal power addresses only shared concerns. That structure is now hollow. Both parties have weaponized federal authority to impose uniform values when in power. Whoever wins elections uses centralized machinery to dominate the opposition. Democracy without federalism is merely elite cycling.

The Federalist Framework Acemoglu Describes

Federalism is the structural requirement for genuine self-government. It rests on two foundational levels of authority.

Community Level

Communities decide their own priorities, values, and cultural norms. This is where experimentation and local variation flourish.

National Level

Federal authority addresses genuinely shared concerns: interstate commerce, national defense, infrastructure serving multiple states, public health affecting all.

Responsiveness

Rules affecting communities must be responsive to those communities' views. Without responsiveness, governance becomes domination.

This creates nondomination—freedom from arbitrary power. Federalism prevents domination by ensuring communities retain authority over what affects them most directly.

"In any society, people must abide by laws, institutions, and norms. The imposition of such rules can be a form of dominance, unless these rules are responsive to the views and priorities of the people bound by them."

The Three Promises: What Liberal Democracy Pledged

Liberal democracy made three specific promises. These are not aspirational but practical bases of legitimacy that allowed democracy to command loyalty.

Drains

Basic infrastructure works reliably—roads, water, sewage, electricity, schools, healthcare. The practical foundation of civilized life.

Shared Prosperity

Economic opportunity is distributed across society, not hoarded by elites. Citizens build material security and pass it to their children.

Voice

Citizens have genuine influence over decisions affecting them. Their concerns are heard by elected officials.

For much of the 20th century, liberal democracy delivered on these three. When it did, it commanded remarkable loyalty. Today, all three are in crisis. It's about federalism's collapse and elite abandonment of self-government.

How Current U.S. Governance Violates Federalism

The Regulatory Overreach Problem

Federal regulatory machinery has expanded far beyond constitutional scope. The promised "drains" are strangled by centralized rule-making that prevents their delivery.

The Infrastructure Paradox

President Biden's infrastructure agenda was strangled by federal regulations his own administration created. Wage controls, antidiscrimination rules, environmental compliance, material specifications. Result: Only 70% of projects began. The federal government defeated its own purpose—it couldn't deliver basic public works because centralized regulatory machinery prevented construction.

The Administrative State Replaces Federalist Deliberation

Educated elites adopted a top-down technocratic approach abandoning federalism's core: that communities should have voice in rules binding them. Decisions flow from bureaucratic expertise imposed on communities regardless of local circumstances or preferences.

The Loss of Voice

85% of Americans believe elected officials don't care what they think—and research confirms they're right. Officials vote along preferences of their wealthiest constituents, ignoring the bottom two-thirds. Communities feel unheard on issues they care about: immigration, curriculum, cultural norms, economic security.

Uniform Values Imposed Top-Down

Rather than permitting regional variation (which federalism enables), educated elites used power in media, academia, and regulation to enforce uniform values across all communities.

School curricula changed without deliberation with affected communities
Immigration policy driven by elite preferences despite 50% public concern
Identity politics became "settled" elite consensus
Dissent was treated as ignorance rather than legitimate variation

The Betrayal: Educated Elites & Social Engineering

As postindustrial society emerged and credentialed professionals gained control of bureaucracies, universities, media, and corporations, something shifted.

Educated elites convinced themselves they should engineer society toward correct values rather than defend basic liberal tenets and let communities self-govern.

They pursued liberal values through top-down social engineering rather than debate and experimentation. They applied a "one-size-fits-all" approach to values that should remain locally variable. This violated liberalism's foundational principle: fallibility—that nobody has perfect knowledge.

The Fatal Irony

Educated elites became a ruling class abandoning liberalism's core commitments. They stopped defending self-government and community voice. They pursued enlightened despotism—imposing what they believed was correct using institutional power rather than persuasion.

Both Parties Abandoned Federalism

This isn't a left-right problem. It's elite behavior across both parties. Each invokes federalism as a weapon against the other, then abandons it the moment they hold power.

The Republican Approach

Republicans invoke "states' rights" when opposing progressive policies. But when they hold power, they use federal authority to enforce uniform outcomes without genuine federalism.

The Progressive Approach

Progressives pursued uniformity through federal regulatory machinery and court intervention. When they held power, they used federal agencies to impose uniform cultural and environmental standards.

This is the ratchet effect: each side, when in power, further erodes federalist constraints.

The Current Imbalance: Republicans Hold the Upper Hand

Republicans currently control the executive branch, Senate, House, and federal courts. This concentration of power enables uniform outcomes without genuine federalism. They can enforce uniform immigration policy, prevent federal environmental regulations, use federal agencies for conservative curriculum, reshape federal courts, and consolidate executive power.

The Danger in This Imbalance

Federal judges appointed now serve for decades. Bureaucratic restructuring creates path dependencies. Each cycle, when used to centralize rather than restore federalism, makes the system more autocratic. Eventually the system becomes a prize to be seized, not a structure to be trusted. That's not liberal democracy. That's elite cycling toward authoritarianism.

The Winning Formula: What Could Break the Cycle

Voters want something neither party offers: working infrastructure, shared prosperity, and genuine voice.

A leader who pursued genuine federalism in service of the three promises would be unstoppable because they would address legitimate grievances, offer structural legitimacy, enable political peace, and restore trust.

This appeals across the spectrum because it's not ideology—it's legitimacy. It says: "I don't need to win a national culture war. I'll focus on what matters materially and let communities decide what they value."

A leader governing through federalism while pursuing the three promises would break the cycle. But it requires accepting outcomes you oppose for the sake of preserving the system.

Why Neither Party Pursues This

It requires surrendering centralized power. Genuine federalism means accepting communities making choices you oppose, not using federal machinery for your values, permitting experimentation and local variation, and constraining yourself when you have power.

No modern political elite has this discipline. They're convinced they know what's good for everyone.

Conclusion: The Path Forward

American voters would reward a leader who pursued genuine federalism in focused pursuit of the three promises. Such a leader would make infrastructure work, restore shared prosperity, ensure genuine voice, and govern through federalism—not imposing uniform values, but protecting community autonomy.

This would be politically revolutionary not because it's radical, but because it would be honest. It says: "The system works when it honors its own structure. Let me restore that structure and deliver on basic promises. You don't need to fight a national culture war—your community can reflect your values."

That message—authentically pursued—would reunite the political center and break the elite cycling hollowing out liberal democracy.

The question is whether any political figure has the courage to pursue it.
