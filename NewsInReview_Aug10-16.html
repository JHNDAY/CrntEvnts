Big MACS Attack

MACS stands for Make American Children Sick. Another contender was MASA, Make America Sick Again, but the MACS reference to McDonald's commercials from nearly thirty years ago was irresistible.

This term refers to the EO Trump issued on Monday which calls for reducing childhood vaccination from 17 to 11 vaccines, eliminating the vaccines for Covid, seasonal flu, hepatitis-A, hepatitis-B, RMV and rotavirus unless a child is at high risk for a given disease. The order also urges states to reconsider which vaccines are required for school attendance. In addition, it requires the MMR vaccine to be split into three separate doses and refers to the new regimen as "gold-standard science". Trump also alluded to children being vaccinated with soda bottles of vaccine, notwithstanding that the doses are no more than 1/10 of a teaspoon. It should also be noted that separate vaccines for measles, mumps and rubella are neither currently available nor licensed in the U.S.. At RFK Jr.'s direction, the CDC attempted to put this new schedule into place in January, but it was blocked by a federal judge; the EO is an attempted end run around the ruling.

It should be noted that states regulate public health and vaccine requirements for schools and employers, so the EO is essentially only advisory in nature. This said, it may put pressure on individual states to change their vaccine recommendations and requirements and likely will reduce vaccine uptake. Moreover, RFK Jr.'s continuing assertions that vaccines cause autism and the CDC's position that this is open to question, have markedly increased vaccine skepticism among the American public. The one study that gave any credence to this link was published in the Lancet in 1998 by Dr. Andrew Wakefield and twelve colleagues based on a sample size of only twelve patients. As subsequent contrary research emerged and Wakefield was then adjudged guilty of fraud, the Lancet withdrew the article and Wakefield's medical accreditation was revoked.

Notwithstanding that any impact of Trump's EO will be indirect, it will have real consequences. In this regard, Dr. Andrew Racine, president of the American Academy of Pediatrics opined "As measles cases reach a 35-year high in the U.S. and with cold and flu season quickly approaching, (the) executive order on vaccines is not only disheartening but dangerous".

The Iran War

On Thursday, JD Vance appeared on Fox News and restated the administration's ever-evolving goals for the war saying that "goal No. 1---keep oil and gas cheap for Americans" and "obviously goal No. 2 is ensure that Iran never gets a nuclear weapon". The first goal then was to undo a new problem which was caused by our war of choice. As for the second goal, the JCPOA, which Trump withdrew from in 2018, had made considerable progress in impeding Iran's development of a nuclear weapon, and Iran apparently showed flexibility on this issue in the negotiations just before we launched the war on February 28.

As the war continues to drag on, so too does the deployment of the USS Abraham Lincoln which left its home port of San Diego over 260 days ago and now has been continuously at sea in the Middle East for 200 days, a modern record for the U.S. Navy. Some families of the 5000 crew members are reporting that they have heard about shortages of supplies, food and toiletries; moldy showers and general disrepair including the flight deck; extremely long hours on duty; and emerging mental health problems among the crew. When reporters confronted Trump with this information, he said "they're not worried" about conditions on the ship and that its deployment had not been "nearly long enough". This said, the USS George Washington is underway in the Strait of Malacca and should arrive in the Middle East in about ten days, presumably to relieve the Lincoln.

Ali Vaez of the International Crisis Group refers to Iran's closure of the Strait of Hormuz as "a sword of Damocles over Trump" and Steven Erlanger noted in the NYT that Iran's intransigence on the Strait and its current excessive demands aim to ensure that we will comply with the terms of the hastily negotiated MOU which strongly favor Iran.

Middle East expert Philip Gordon of Brookings, who served as National Security Advisor to Kamala Harris, aptly summarizes where things stand, writing "U.S. acceptance of Iranian control over the Strait of Hormuz is an extraordinary outcome only made conceivable by an administration that squandered legitimacy, alienated allies, attempted regime change in a war it had not adequately prepared for and that is being forced to accept a catastrophic outcome without viable alternatives".

This week, details emerged about how Trump surreptitiously switched planes last month as his retinue left the NATO meeting in Turkey. The switch was in response to Israeli intelligence that Iran plotted to shoot down the non-Qatari Air Force One with shoulder-fired missiles which the CIA found to be highly dubious, an understandable assessment given how faulty the Israeli intelligence which prompted us to go to war had been. After boarding Air Force One, Trump was secretly spirited off the plane and transferred to a military plane on a food catering truck. Apparently he was accompanied on the truck by his executive assistant Natalie Harp, former valet/stolen classified document concealer and current go-for aide Walt Nauta, Deputy Chief-of-Staff Dan Scavino, the indispensable, well-groomed and brilliant military strategist Pete Hegseth, and, presumably, a large supply of Diet Coke. This Week's Election News: Two Closely Watched Races

WI Democratic Gubernatorial Primary: After popular 2-term governor Tony Evers decided not to run, his lieutenant governor Sara Rodriguez was viewed as the favorite and the likely Democrat to run against Trump-endorsed conservative Rep. Tom Tiffany. However, when major financial problems surfaced for the Rodriguez campaign which led to the firing of her campaign manager, Rodriguez felt compelled to drop out of the race in mid-July. The next day, Governor Evers and WI party insiders induced Milwaukee County Executive David Crowley to reenter the race as an alternative to outspoken DSA member Assemblywoman Francesca Hong who had previously called on social media for cancelling Thanksgiving, defunding the police and abolishing ICE and the Senate. When Hong's prior social media postings expressing extreme views came to light, she weakly responded "These are beliefs I held when I was in different positions myself, and right now I'm in the position of running for governor". By way of contrast, Tony Evers said in his endorsement that Crowley was "not just a candidate who can win in November, but he's the kind of person who cares about doing the job right".

Although they had supported progressive non-DSA member Abdul El-Sayed in the MI senatorial primary, AOC and Bernie Sanders refrained from endorsing Hong. Notwithstanding her extreme views and the lack of key endorsements, Hong still led Crowley by 20% or more in the polling which stopped a week before the primary. It was a stunning upset when Crowley went on to beat Hong by a scant 0.5%, just 3800 votes out of the nearly 800k cast.

A week earlier, progressive firebrand Abdul El-Sayed defeated traditional Democratic Rep. Haley Stevens in the MI senatorial primary by a narrow 1.0% margin after also leading by double digits in polling. Juxtaposing his win over Zohran Mamdani winning the NYC mayoral election last year and three DSA members, Darializa Avila Chevalier in NY, Melat Kiros in CO and Donavan McKinney in MI defeating three traditional liberal House incumbents in primaries this year, the media has been suggesting that such candidates were the wave of the future for the Democrats and that the party was in turmoil. When we step back and note that Mamdani is a gifted politician and El-Sayed is quite impressive and charismatic as well, and that the mayoral and three DSA House wins were in ultra-Blue urban venues, it does not appear that these four DSA-adjacent (El-Sayed is not a member) wins augur the future for Democrats. In this context, when a perfect political storm put Francesca Hong in a great position to win, she still lost to a more qualified traditional candidate, who had to relaunch his campaign less than four weeks before the election, because she was not a good candidate. And in MI with its influential Arab population, the impressive El-Sayed barely prevailed against the well-qualified, but uncharismatic, Stevens and may even have lost if the campaign extended just another week. In both of these statewide elections, the better candidate prevailed; in the author's view, the punditry would be well-served to avoid universal generalizations.

MN Democratic Senatorial Primary: When Senator Tina Smith decided not to run for a second full term, two leading candidates quickly emerged to replace her—centrist Democrat Rep. Angie Craig, endorsed by Nancy Pelosi, Hakeem Jeffries and several senators—and progressive Lt. Gov. Peggy Flanagan, endorsed by Bernie Sanders, Tina Smith and Elizabeth Warren. Flanagan, who is completing her eighth year as Tim Walz' Lt. Gov. criticized Craig for her acceptance of contributions from corporate donors, her strong support for Israel and her vote for the Trump-backed Laken Riley Act which became a real liability after the shootings of Renee Good and Alex Pretti. She advocated for Medicare-for-all, replacing ICE, blocking military aid to Israel and universal child care. Whereas Craig has lived in MN for 21 years and has never held statewide office, Flanagan is a lifelong MN resident, has won two statewide elections running with Tim Walz, and has a 32% net positive favorability rating. Although Craig does not embrace all of Flanagan's positions, they agree on more issues than they differ on. But in a change election, especially resonant in MN after the immigration crackdown in the Twin Cities, Flanagan's populism prevailed and she beat Craig by a 20% margin. Flanagan will be running against former TV sports reporter Michele Tafoya who has no political experience and is less circumspect than Flanagan.
