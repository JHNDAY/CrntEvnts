A Quick Word About Polling Efficacy

Given how Trump won in 2016 after the pre-election polls favored Hillary Clinton by about 5%, and again in 2024 after Kamala Harris led the polls by about 1%, the efficacy of the polls has come into question. In this context, an American Association for Public Opinion Research (AAPOR) study found the aggregate presidential polling to overstate the Democratic candidate's vote share by 3.1% in 2016, 4.6% in 2020, and 2.7% in 2024. However, AAPOR found the polling for the two most recent midterms to be very accurate, as the polls only overstated the Democrats' congressional vote in 2018 by 0.1% and the Republicans' vote by 0.6% in 2022.

At best, the Democrats would have 22 trifectas vs. 20 for the Republicans; at worst the tally would be 15 for the Democrats vs. 23 for the Republicans.

Election Tampering Efforts

Living up to his "If you ain't cheating, you ain't trying" ethic, Trump and the DOJ have been hard at work trying to intervene in the elections to benefit the Republicans. Here's how these efforts have played out over the last few days:

  • A week ago Friday, U.S. District Judge Sparkle Sooknanan blocked the federal government from using the flawed SAVE database to review state voter registration lists to identify any so-called non-citizens for potential removal from the voter rolls. The judge wrote that the audit plan would run "roughshod over long-standing laws that were carefully crafted to protect the rights of the American people... Behind all the window dressing, the challenged policy represents the federal government's attempt to decide who gets to vote." Her ruling bars the 18 states that had already submitted their data to the DOJ from using the SAVE database to cull their voter rolls.
  • On Thursday, the DOJ sued California for not having promptly reviewed a list it received two days earlier of 20,000 suspected non-citizens who received mail-in ballots in Los Angeles County.
  • Then on Friday, the DOJ warned that any California election workers who knowingly count ballots submitted by suspected non-citizens would be subject to criminal investigation and possible prosecution.
"President Trump will use any tool, legal or not, to try and make sure he gets the results he wants this year." Senator Mark Warner (D-VA)

Notwithstanding that the federal judiciary has largely countered voter suppression initiatives, Republican-administered states have greater flexibility to cull the voting rolls, limit mail-in and early voting, and reduce the number of voting machines in Democratic voting precincts. In this regard, just this week a Tarrant County (includes Fort Worth and Irving) district judge denied a TRO which would have reopened 92 newly closed polling places.
